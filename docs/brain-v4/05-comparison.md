# 05. Claude pass vs Codex pass: comparison and resolution

Claude researched `01-what-is-possible.md` while Codex ran three independent
tracks (`research/codex/R4-realism.md`, `R5-mind.md`, `R6-senses.md`) from the
briefs in `research/codex/briefs/`. Codex never saw Claude's conclusions.
This file records where they agreed, what each found alone, where they
disagreed and how it was settled, and what changed in the other docs as a
result. Every settled fact was checked against the source text
(`research/abstracts.md`, regenerated by `tools/fetch_abstracts.py`), not
against either pass's summary.

## Agreements (both passes, independently)

1. Short-form humanlikeness is already achieved at frontier scale, depends
   heavily on the persona prompt, and is not evidence of a mind. Neither
   pass would use a Turing-style pass rate as an acceptance gate.
2. Functional affect, drives, inner life and identity are ENGINEERING.
   Whether any of it is felt is UNKNOWABLE and must never be an acceptance
   criterion.
3. Consciousness indicator properties (Butlin et al. 2023) are worth
   auditing as architecture, as a report, never as a milestone.
4. Bottom-up brain emulation is not a route (SCIENCE + RESOURCE).
5. Reading emotion from faces is limited by science, not cameras (Barrett et
   al. 2019): weak, context-conditioned evidence only.
6. Learning inside the weights is SCIENCE + RESOURCE on this hardware;
   external memory stays primary.
7. W2 (the user's words moving affect) is the first dependency. Driving
   prosody or anything else from today's affect state would faithfully
   express a broken signal.
8. Identity needs an invariant core plus slow, corroborated change, with the
   same human numbers (Roberts & DelVecchio 2000; Roberts, Walton &
   Viechtbauer 2006).

## Found only by Claude, kept

- Park et al. 2023: memory, reflection and planning raised believability
  (TrueSkill 29.89 full vs 21.21 none; human crowdworkers 22.95), with the
  same language model in every agent condition. The strongest evidence that
  architecture, not only the model, drives perceived believability.
- Park et al. 2024 (current version): agents built from two-hour interviews
  reach 83-86% of people's own two-week test-retest consistency.
- De Freitas et al.: "feeling heard" is the main mechanism behind loneliness
  reduction.
- Strachan et al. 2024: frontier-model theory of mind is at human level on
  most tasks; the gap is what the architecture does with it (`NF-26`).
- Stivers et al. 2009's exact numbers (mean +208 ms, mode 0, language means
  +7 to +469 ms), checked in the full text.
- Chalmers 2023 alongside Butlin et al.

## Found by neither pass until the source check

- **Jones & Bergen's PNAS version (May 2026) adds a third study with
  15-minute conversations.** Two persona-prompted models reached 56% and
  59%, down from 73% at five minutes. Longer exposure weakens the illusion.
  This is direct support for V4's long-horizon focus: short-form realism is
  not the hard part. Adopted into `01` A.

## Candidate V3 finding (raised by this comparison)

`backend/app/vision/reflex.py` calls a smile "unambiguously positive" and a
brow furrow "unambiguously negative-valenced". Its module docstring also says
the live camera wiring "is intentionally not part of this change", which is
stale: `backend/app/vision/agent.py` now calls `score_blendshapes`, and
`brain_agent.py` applies the result to the agent's affect.

The mechanism itself (the agent's mood nudged by the user's expression) is
defensible as emotional contagion. The "unambiguously" labels contradict
Barrett et al. 2019, which shows facial configurations vary across cultures,
situations and people and can go with more than one emotion. It belongs in
V3's findings ledger with an owner (W2 owns affect inputs). Not yet filed:
it is recorded here so it is not lost.

## Decisions D1-D4 (decided 2026-09-27; answers in `06-decisions.md`)

- **D1. Perceived feeling: goal or risk?** V4's target is a mind people
  experience as real, including depth. Codex argues that inflating people's
  belief that it feels things could harm some users; the evidence for that
  is thin (one 21-day study, no overall effect, anthropomorphism as a
  mediator of reported social impact, dependence unmeasured). Is higher
  perceived Experience something V4 aims for, or something it keeps within a
  bound once a dependence measure exists?
- **D2. External human studies.** Is a recruited cohort (about 120 people,
  ethics review, weeks of use) in scope for V4, or does V4 stay with an
  internal rater tier and make no external claims?
- **D3. Model tier.** Which generation models V4 is allowed to run on
  (local only, larger local, frontier through local Claude Code).
- **D4. Disclosure.** DR-001's immutable core includes honesty. Confirm the
  mind always says it is an AI when asked, and never claims human memories
  or a human body, however real it seems.

## Search before building: existing "realism" scripts in the repo

`scripts/research/human_realism_eval.py` (924 lines) and
`scripts/research/human_fidelity_test.py` (113 lines) predate V3. Neither
measures perceived realism:
- `human_realism_eval.py` times NATS round trips and Neo4j traversals and
  plots a stored PAD trajectory. Its "paralinguistic realism" module derives
  numbers from other benchmark files and falls back to hard-coded constants
  when those files are missing (`intent_accuracy = 85.70`,
  `redis_fetch = 0.164`), so it can print realism figures that no run
  measured.
- `human_fidelity_test.py` subscribes to state and output subjects on a live
  mesh and collects PAD values and paralinguistic tags.

No human judgment is involved in either. So the claim in `01`/`03` that
nothing measures perceived realism holds. The fallback constants make the
first script a candidate for V3's pre-existing-problem sweep (SW): a
research script that can report numbers nobody measured. V4-1 builds the
eval fresh and does not reuse either.
