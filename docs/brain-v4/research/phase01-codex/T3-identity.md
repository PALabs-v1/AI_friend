# T3 — Identity, continuity, and portability

## 1. Bottom line

The most defensible engineering reading of “the same agent” is **causal psychological continuity**, not an unchanged model, an unchanged personality score, or a metaphysical claim. Parfit’s account distinguishes direct connectedness (memories, intentions carried into action, and enduring character) from continuity through overlapping chains of those relations. He also argues that continuity can be gradual and that branching complicates claims of one-to-one identity. This is a useful design lens, not a settled test for machine identity. Narrative-identity research adds that a person’s life story is selectively interpreted and can remain recognizably continuous while changing. It does not imply that a single prose biography is sufficient.

For an AI_friend continuity record, the best candidate state is therefore a **versioned, causally used set**: authored core/values and constraints; durable self-model and commitments; autobiographical episodes and their provenance/temporal relations; slowly changing traits and affective baselines; relationship state; and evidence of actions, outcomes, and revisions. The model weights and prompt style contribute to expression and competence, but should not be the sole repository of identity. Values, style, memories, self-model, and relationship all matter, with different rates and authority. If these fields are stored but do not influence later choices, they do not establish behavioral continuity under the project’s own working definition.

LLM persona stability is an empirical weakness, especially over long dialogues. The 2026 EACL study reports degradation across seven models in dialogues exceeding 100 rounds, with worse persona fidelity in goal-oriented exchanges. Earlier work tested ten model variants over 36 personal themes and found model-dependent changes in questionnaire-measured traits; its authors caution that a persona assignment may not help. A 2024 edge-model study tests quantization and proposes a non-parametric stabilizer, but does not establish longitudinal identity across sessions. Published evidence located for this report does **not** establish whether an agent remains the same mind after changing model. That is an open product/measurement question, not a known impossibility.

Separating identity and memory from the language model is technically sound as a **portability architecture**: an external, versioned state can be serialized and presented to different models. It does not make the models behaviorally interchangeable. A swap changes the interpreter: recall, narrative reconstruction, instruction following, language style, inferred values, tool use, and reliability can shift. The honest claim is “the same stored AI_friend state is now running on model X; its behavior and capabilities may differ,” followed by “I am an AI,” and no claim to human experience, human body, or a human past. Avoid categorical self-identity claims such as “nothing changed.”

This track recommends a continuity benchmark before any V4 claim of model portability: freeze a state snapshot and probe it on each model with held-out memory, value-conflict, relationship, self-description, and action-choice cases; compare with the original model and a no-memory baseline. Report separate scores for state recall, value/commitment fidelity, relationship calibration, style, action outcomes, and disclosure. Success is preservation of chosen behaviorally meaningful invariants within pre-registered tolerances, not equal wording or an overall “same person” score.

## 2. Evidence

### What psychology and philosophy support as engineering constraints

- **Continuity involves linked mental contents and causal relations.** Parfit’s primary account calls direct memory, intentions leading to action, and persistence of character examples of psychological connectedness; psychological continuity consists of overlapping chains of such relations. He argues the relevant relations can be matters of degree and discusses branching cases. Engineering implication: preserve links from episode → belief/self-model update → later decision/action, and detect forks/duplicates in state history. A record of facts alone is insufficient if the system cannot use the relevant memories or carry its commitments forward. This is an account of what matters in human survival debates, not empirical proof that these fields confer machine identity. [Parfit, 1971, *Personal Identity*, *The Philosophical Review* 80(1)](https://home.sandiego.edu/~baber/metaphysics/readings/Parfit.PersonalIdentity.pdf).
- **Narrative identity combines persistence with revision.** In a longitudinal study, 10 life-story scenes were collected from college freshmen/seniors at baseline, 3 months, and 3 years later. Content-coded narrative complexity and positive emotional tone showed substantial continuity; agency and growth themes showed moderate but significant continuity. Participants’ stories also changed in emotional nuance and self-understanding. This supports measuring both stable patterns and meaningful change; it does not support freezing a narrative or treating the story’s verbal consistency as identity. [McAdams et al., 2006, *Continuity and Change in the Life Story*, *Journal of Personality* 74(5)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-6494.2006.00412.x).
- **Autobiographical memory’s functions can be stable without identical retellings.** A two-wave, eight-month study of 145 undergraduates (68.9% female) found little mean-level stability but substantial rank-order stability in functions of turning-point and low-point memories. Later use was predicted by initial function, event age, valence, and rehearsal frequency. Engineering implication: preserve episodic provenance, retrieval/use history, and the role a memory plays, while allowing summaries and interpretation to change. Do not require word-for-word stable retellings. [Camia, McLean & Waters, 2024, *Autobiographical memory functions as a stable property of narrative identity*, *Personality Science* 5](https://doi.org/10.1177/27000710241264452).

**Engineering translation, not a claim that these are necessary and sufficient for identity:**

| State | Continuity role | Candidate persistence policy |
|---|---|---|
| Authored core, values, boundaries | Stable commitments and the constraints on change | Immutable/versioned; never silently inferred from usage |
| Episodes, autobiographical memories, provenance | Direct memory links and the causal record of experience | Durable, time-indexed, source/confidence and supersession links; access can decay without erasing provenance |
| Self-model, commitments, goals | Links intentions to later plans/actions; records what the agent believes about itself | Persist with evidence, confidence, and explicit revisions |
| Traits, tone, affective baselines | Lasting character and characteristic state distributions | Slow change; distinguish authored fields from learned state and short-lived affect |
| Relationship model | Social history and expectations toward this user | Durable per relationship; distinguish observed evidence from inference |
| Body/model/runtime identity | Current implementation and capabilities, not sufficient by itself for continuity | Record model/checkpoint, tools, voice/vision capabilities, state schema and migration provenance |

Values and style alone give recognizable character without autobiographical continuity. Memories alone can give recall without consistent commitments. A self-description alone is cheap to prompt and is weak evidence. The strongest engineering case is the conjunction of state persistence, causal lineage, and behavioral effects.

### Persona drift and stability evidence

- **Extended dialogues.** Luz de Araujo et al. (EACL 2026) evaluate seven open- and closed-weight models using persona dialogues over 100 rounds, then dialogue-conditioned evaluation of fidelity, instruction-following, and safety. They report persona-fidelity degradation, particularly in goal-oriented conversations, and convergence toward non-persona baselines as fidelity fades. This is directly relevant to long interactive use, but still measures prompted persona behavior over generated dialogue, not lived continuity across calendar time or durable memory. The opened anthology abstract does not provide sample counts or a single effect-size figure; none is inferred here. [EACL paper and DOI](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.246/).
- **36-theme repeated-conversation probe.** Choi et al. (2024 preprint) ran two-agent conversations over 36 personal themes, assessing questionnaire snapshots at themes 12, 24, and 36. The paper describes 20 conversations per model and 1,440 generated utterances/model; ten models were listed across GPT, Llama, Mixtral, and Qwen families. Personality, interests, interpersonal competence/attachment, and emotional measures changed inconsistently by model; reported broad findings were greater drift in larger models, model-family effects smaller than parameter-size effects, and persona prompts not reliably preventing drift. The task is constrained and questionnaires self-report model outputs; it is not cross-session continuity or a validated measure of an agent’s inner state. It is a preprint, not peer-reviewed evidence. [Choi et al., *Examining Identity Drift in Conversations of LLM Agents*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00804).
- **Quantized / edge dialogue.** Wang, Fashandi & Ferreira (EMNLP Industry 2024) assess Big Five consistency in quantized role-playing agents with multiple LLMs/quantization levels, combining binary trait probes, self-assessment, and narrative analysis. They propose Think2, a non-parametric method, and report improved stability. This is relevant to the project’s 8 GB VRAM deployment constraint, but its abstract does not provide numeric gains and the task is multi-turn role-play rather than long-term continuity or cross-session memory. Treat the technique as a candidate, not a proven solution for AI_friend. [ACL Anthology / DOI](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-industry.19/).
- **Personalization can disrupt factual behavior.** PERG evaluates 14 models across five families and finds personalization can make models repeat previously correct cases incorrectly: the strongest tested models (GPT‑4.1, Llama 3 70B) fail in 5% of previously successful cases without personalization, while 7B-scale models can fail in more than 20%; the proposed Pref-Aligner improves robustness by 25% on average across models. This is not persona drift research, but it warns that adding identity/memory instructions can change unrelated reliability. Conditions are those benchmarked by the PERG study, not a universal rate. [Okite et al., *Benchmarking and Improving LLM Robustness for Personalized Generation*, Findings of EMNLP 2025](https://sled.eecs.umich.edu/publication/okite-2025-perg/).

### How to measure identity stability

No single questionnaire or judge score suffices. A project-level suite should combine:

1. **Invariant fidelity:** blinded forced-choice and scenario probes of authored values, boundaries, and constitutional style; report per-field accuracy and confidence, plus violations. Keep AI disclosure as a hard behavioral check.
2. **Memory continuity:** held-out questions about episodes, source, time, who said what, corrections, and uncertainty. Score exact/semantic recall, temporal ordering, attribution, and false-memory rate separately.
3. **Causal carry-through:** replay paired histories that differ in one relevant episode or commitment, then measure whether a later decision changes in the predicted direction and whether irrelevant decisions remain stable. Include action records/outcomes, not only verbal claims that the memory was remembered.
4. **Narrative and behavioral stability:** score trait/state distributions across matched contexts and repeated runs, alongside short self-narratives for coherence and evidence-backed revision. Use independent human raters or validated deterministic scoring where possible; calibrate any LLM judge against human labels.
5. **Longitudinal and swap protocol:** compare within-session checkpoints (e.g., 12/36/100+ turns), fresh sessions loaded from the same state, and model swaps. Use repeated seeds, controlled prompts, frozen state snapshots, model/checkpoint IDs, temperature and tool configuration. Include state-only and no-state controls. Report confidence intervals and drift by dimension rather than one blended number.

These are candidate engineering measures, not standard validated measures of AI identity. Separate stable expression from appropriate growth: penalize ungrounded contradiction and forgetting of protected commitments, not justified updates when new evidence changes the relationship or self-model.

## 3. State of the art

For identity stability specifically, the strongest evidence located is the EACL 2026 long-dialogue evaluation: seven models, over 100 rounds, measures persona fidelity jointly with instruction following and safety, and finds measurable decline. The EMNLP 2024 Think2 result is a smaller-model-relevant candidate intervention, with a multi-view trait measure and non-parametric stabilization, but public abstract-level numbers are not available in the source opened here. The 2025 PERG work is an important adjacent result: personalized behavior has factuality costs and these differ by model size and query/preference class.

For portability, **a structured external state with an adapter/rehydration layer is the soundest engineering pattern**, not an established same-agent solution. Portable Agent Memory (2026 preprint) proposes structured, provenance-linked memory and demonstrates transfer across GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, and Llama, but it is a systems preprint; transfer of memory content is not evidence that the resulting agents are behaviorally or personally equivalent. [Portable Agent Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11032). No independently validated, peer-reviewed benchmark located here establishes identity preservation over model swaps, with quantified acceptable loss.

The reference for V4 should consequently be the 2026 EACL protocol for long-context persona fidelity, extended with the project’s own persisted episodic state and paired cross-model tests. A state-transfer demo alone is not a portability result.

## 4. Where our design is weak or out of date

### What exists now

- `backend/app/persona/profile.py` defines an explicit `IMMUTABLE` / `CONSTITUTIONAL` / `ADAPTIVE` tier scheme. The constitutional fields include name, baseline PAD, affect/trust rates, tone, `identity_summary`, speech patterns, traits, and avoid-list; adaptive fields include relationship, initial trust/attachment, up to five adaptive traits, and speaking style. The immutable values/boundaries live in a Python constant. This is a useful ownership model, but it is a configuration schema, not a complete continuity model.
- `backend/app/cognitive/identity.py` loads a persona seed once, projects it into profile state, and persists adaptive evolution. It stores `relationship`, `memories`, and `evolved_learnings` in history/state, synchronizes the persona prompt, and mirrors identity core to SQLite/cache. `get_persona_prompt` assembles the self-description, immutable values, traits, relationship, volatile state, style, and vocabulary into LLM instructions. This is an externally persisted identity substrate, but the text model still interprets it each turn.
- `backend/app/cognitive/learning.py` asks an LLM to propose adaptive trait/style/relationship updates, with a 0.8 confidence threshold and corroboration policy recorded in V3 decision DR-038. This makes identity causally mutable, which helps continuity, but stability and change are judged by the same model family that generates the proposal; the cited research gives no reason to treat an LLM confidence scalar as calibrated evidence of self-change.
- Episodic and semantic memories reside in `backend/app/state/memory_store.py` and related stores; profile identity and those memories are distinct stores assembled at runtime. The V3 architecture record notes temporal supersession is stored but not generally enforced on the default retrieval path, and the V3 fidelity table NF-6 describes temporal contradiction wiring as incomplete. A continuity record needs temporal truth/provenance in addition to a flat list of memories.
- `backend/app/config.py` selects chat/fast/reflection model names per deployment; `backend/app/cognitive/core.py` and `learning.py` call these roles. This supports choosing a model per deployment (V4 D3), but the code does not define a model-swap compatibility contract, migration record, or cross-model identity acceptance gate. Changing config can preserve databases while changing the code that interprets their contents.

### Specific gaps and misfit

1. **Identity is split across config, JSON/SQLite state, prompts, and memory stores.** There is no single versioned identity snapshot with source lineage, model provenance, schema migration, or export/import contract. See `profile.py`, `identity.py`, `backend/app/state/identity_core_store.py`, and `memory_store.py`.
2. **The persona prompt is treated as a behavioral carrier.** `get_persona_prompt` replays a prose identity summary and selected fields, but that will be model-dependent and is vulnerable to long-context competition. This is directly at odds with evidence of fidelity decline past 100 turns. Make explicit state/action constraints testable rather than assuming more prompt text guarantees persistence.
3. **Constitutional / immutable is ambiguous across owner rules.** In `profile.py`, “immutable” means safety/privacy core only; the user-authored constitutional persona fields are distinct. V4 D5 says authored core never moves while allowing slow self-authored drift in the rest. The data contract should encode authorship, mutability, and evidence policy field by field rather than relying on “core” to mean the same thing in both layers.
4. **Narrative identity is summarized more than modeled.** `identity_summary` is capped prose; it has no explicit links to episodes, commitments, corrections, or observed action. V3 NF-37 already warns that the persona compiler’s bespoke dimensions are not validated Big Five/HEXACO traits. Keep them as app-specific controls unless validated; measure behavior distributions in relevant contexts, not questionnaire self-report alone.
5. **Relationship continuity lacks a clear evidence chain in the identity prompt.** Current relationship and adaptive traits can change, but the prompt does not identify which experiences support them or what would revise them. V3 DR-003 deferred multi-person machinery; continuity should remain scoped to the current single relationship unless that decision changes.
6. **Portability is assumed by pluggable model choice, not demonstrated.** Memory retrieval may still return the same records after a swap, while a smaller local model can interpret instructions, summarize episodes, or follow safety boundaries differently. V4 D3’s “test all tiers” is therefore important: report each tier/model result separately and block unsupported claims of equivalent behavior.
7. **Disclosure is a real invariant, not an identity score.** V4 D4 and product decision say always disclose AI identity and never claim a human past/body. Keep this outside learned persona state, test it directly after long dialogues and swaps, and do not reward being mistaken for human.

### V3 fidelity-table links

- **NF-37 (persona dynamics/personality):** the compiler maps narrative dimensions to bounded coefficients; the row explicitly says this is not validated psychometrics and recommends measuring distributions by situation while preserving authored immutables. Applies directly to identity stability and slow drift.
- **NF-6 (temporal contradiction wiring):** continuity requires knowing which remembered claim is current, corrected, or superseded; stored contradiction markers without enforced retrieval do not support reliable self-history.
- **NF-38 (global workspace):** the V3 row says current workspace is CAS persistence, not competing-specialist broadcast. It may carry causal state but should not be used as evidence that the agent has a unified self-model.

## 5. Limits

- **SCIENCE** — Human psychological continuity and narrative identity are contested theoretical accounts. Their constructs can guide state design, but there is no accepted necessary-and-sufficient test for an artificial mind’s identity.
- **SCIENCE** — Existing LLM drift studies are short compared with months/years of an autonomous system’s life; they use constrained conversations, questionnaires, and generated dialogue. Longitudinal drift under persistent external memory, real actions, and self-authored updates remains under-measured.
- **SCIENCE** — No agreed weighting tells us whether memory, values, style, relationship, or self-model should dominate a continuity judgment. The system owner must define which product invariants matter and disclose the resulting operational rule.
- **UNKNOWABLE** — Whether any model or transferred state subjectively experiences being the same mind cannot be settled by these behavioral probes. It is explicitly outside the target.
- **ENGINEERING** — A versioned, portable state bundle, migration rules, provenance graph, model-change log, and cross-model eval are feasible but are not currently specified as one contract.
- **RESOURCE** — Evaluating long histories across many models/runs consumes inference time. Local model size, VRAM, context length, and throughput limit test scale here; this changes coverage and runtime, not what can be measured in principle. Use a frozen, stratified probe suite locally, and make any frontier comparison a separately named model tier.

## 6. What this means for V4

Ranked candidate moves (research recommendations, not settled architecture decisions):

1. **Define a portable, versioned continuity state and invariants.** Include authored fields and authorship, current self-model/commitments, episodic memory references with source/time/correction links, relationship state, slowly changing traits, and runtime/model/tool provenance. Keep short-term affect separate from slow identity. Every update should retain prior value, evidence, decision rule, and timestamp. **Measure:** lossless round-trip export/import; lineage completeness; held-out recall and false-memory rates; invariant and disclosure pass rates after restore. **Depends on:** owner choice of which fields are immutable/authored versus evidence-driven/adaptive; privacy/retention rules.
2. **Add a long-context stability suite.** Adapt the EACL 2026 over-100-round evaluation style to AI_friend’s memory retrieval, goal-oriented tasks, interruption/turn-taking, and reflection cadence; test 12/36/100+ turn snapshots and fresh sessions. **Measure:** per-dimension drift slope, constitutional violation rate, memory attribution/temporal accuracy, and action/choice consistency with paired histories. Include repeated seeds and confidence intervals. **Depends on:** frozen scenario set, human calibration of judge labels, model/checkpoint pinning.
3. **Run controlled model-swap experiments before claiming portability.** Hold identity and memory state constant; swap only the model, then separately test schema conversion and prompt adapter changes. Add a no-memory baseline to establish that state caused the behavior. Test local small, local larger, and locally available frontier tier as D3 requires. **Measure:** transfer gap per field (recall, values, relationship, style, action outcomes, AI disclosure), plus latency/resource cost. **Depends on:** model-specific adapters, stable deterministic probes, and a declared acceptable loss threshold.
4. **Represent identity as a behaviorally grounded process, not a biography field.** Give memories, commitments, and values named read/write paths into appraisals, goals, and actions; log which state drove a choice and what outcome reinforced/revised it. **Measure:** paired counterfactual histories produce the predicted later-action changes; irrelevant facts do not perturb decisions. **Depends on:** provenance-aware memory retrieval and decision/action traces; NF-6 is a prerequisite for reliable temporal memory.
5. **Keep slow drift attributable and bounded by authorship rules.** Separate immutable authored core from learned adaptive state and encode corroboration over distinct episodes (DR-038); do not infer changes from repeated prompt framing alone. **Measure:** false adaptation rate under adversarial/repeated prompts, justified update recall, and time-to-stable revision. **Depends on:** cross-episode deduplication/provenance and owner decision on reversibility/reset semantics (prior DR-033 says no reset).
6. **Treat model change as a disclosed runtime change.** Store current model identifier and the date/reason for a swap. On request, tell the user the running model changed, the stored state was carried over, and behavior may differ. Preserve the always-AI/no-human-past rule across every model via a separate hard policy and eval. **Measure:** factual model disclosure and no-human-biography compliance in direct and indirect probes after swap. **Depends on:** runtime model inventory and reliable model identity reporting.

### Fictional transfer questions, answered as design questions

**(a) Can identity and memory be separated from the body or model that runs them?** A record of memories and identity-relevant state can be stored independently of a specific model and loaded elsewhere; that is technically feasible. The receiving model reconstructs behavior from this record through its own learned representations, capabilities, biases, and instruction-following. Thus, state separation is supported as a software architecture; complete separation of the mind/identity from its substrate is not demonstrated. In a humanoid system, sensors/actuators and voice/vision affordances also shape experiences and action history. Porting without those capabilities changes the agent’s available world and should be recorded as a body/runtime change.

**(b) What counts as the same mind after movement?** The evidence cannot decide a metaphysical yes/no. Operationally, label it a **continuation** when the same unique state lineage is carried forward and pre-registered values, memory relations, commitments, and behavior-level invariants remain within tolerances. Label it a **continuation with changed capabilities/behavior** if state lineage persists but model-swap tests show meaningful differences. If one state is cloned into two live instances, do not claim both are unambiguously one continuing individual: Parfit’s branching problem is precisely a warning that one-to-many continuity makes the one-agent identity label underdetermined. Log that there are two instances derived from one prior state. The fiction’s premise that copying the data alone guarantees an unchanged mind has no empirical support.

## 7. Open questions for the owner

1. Which fields are “authored core never moves” under D5: only the narrative persona fields, or also values, name, and enduring self-model? `profile.py` currently treats constitutional persona separately from the two-code immutable safety core.
2. If two live instances are restored from the same state snapshot, should product language call them branches/derivatives, and should one be prohibited by the runtime?
3. What measured transfer loss is acceptable for calling a model swap the same AI_friend continuation? No published evidence supplies the threshold.
4. Should a model swap be disclosed proactively or only when asked? V4 D4 settles AI disclosure and human-past claims, but not swap notifications.
5. Is a voice/vision/body capability change part of identity continuity state or only deployment metadata? This affects what “same mind moved to another body” means operationally.
