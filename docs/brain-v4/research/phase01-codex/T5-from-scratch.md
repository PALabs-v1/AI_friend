# T5 — A model built from scratch for a human-like mind

**Research date:** 2026-10-28
**Framing:** Every model available to the project is a test vehicle. The owner's stated end goal is a model trained from scratch to behave like a human. This replaces V4 D3's 2026-09-27 per-deployment-model-choice framing for this track. It does not change the always-disclose rule or the authored persona with a fixed core and slow drift.

## 1. Bottom line

The research supports a **small, purpose-built experiment**, not the claim that a human-like model is easier to build from scratch. “Much easier” is **contradicted as a claim about the whole project**: a bespoke objective and recurrent state may be easier to align with this architecture than adapting a frontier model, but the scratch model first has to acquire language, social pragmatics, world knowledge, and robust behavior from vastly less data and compute than established foundation models. The literature demonstrates narrow language competence and narrow task advantages under carefully curated data. It does not demonstrate that a sub-1B model trained from scratch can match a general model on persistent, human-like behavior across situations.

Scaling laws are useful for forecasting held-out next-token loss in their calibrated regime. They do not forecast personality, stable autobiographical memory, social judgment, initiative, or human-like longitudinal behavior. The 2026 BabyLM controlled study is a useful update: across 432 runs under 10M- and 100M-word budgets, autoregressive Transformers led the evaluated alternatives on the public benchmark suite, while masked language modeling was the strongest non-autoregressive baseline. That supports systematic small-data research, not a general “human learning” result. A 2025 BabyLM pragmatic study found models trained on under 100M tokens still below both child and 3T-token model performance on its pragmatic benchmark.

The most defensible near-term project is a **10–30M parameter recurrent or small Transformer language model** trained on **10–30M carefully curated tokens**, with a small number of isolated objectives and a longitudinal interaction benchmark. At roughly 6·N·T training FLOPs, 30M parameters over 30M tokens is about 5.4·10^15 FLOPs before overhead. It is plausible on the stated 8 GB GPU/M5 machine, but actual wall time depends on measured training throughput and memory behavior; budget days, not hours, until a benchmark says otherwise. It can demonstrate a working tokenizer, training pipeline, state interface, and data-efficiency curve. It cannot establish broad human-like behavior, nor does it put the model on a path to frontier capability without radically more data and compute.

V4 should therefore make the **brain independent of any one language model**. Persist typed state and interaction history outside the model; define versioned, model-neutral contracts for generation, structured appraisal, embeddings, memory reads/writes, and action proposals; log which state fields causally changed an action. That preserves a future route to scratch-built language models without waiting for one to exist. The near-term empirical question is not “is this mind human?” but whether a specified state variable persists, changes a later behavior under controlled intervention, and improves a relevant measure.

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 Scale, data, and what the scaling laws establish

Kaplan et al. (2020) measured cross-entropy loss while varying parameter count, dataset size, and compute over trends spanning more than six orders of magnitude. Their central result is smooth power-law improvement when model size and data scale together, with diminishing returns when one is fixed. They also report weak sensitivity to depth-versus-width within the tested range. The target was language-model loss, not human-like social behavior. [Kaplan et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)

Hoffmann et al. (2022) trained more than 400 language models ranging from 70M to over 16B parameters on 5–500B tokens, then fit a compute-allocation rule. Their 70B Chinchilla model used about 1.4T tokens and matched Gopher's compute budget while outperforming it on the reported model evaluations. The rule is often summarized as scaling parameters and training tokens together. This is evidence about compute-optimal next-token pretraining under their data and training setup, not a universal law of cognition or a guarantee for repeated small corpora. [Hoffmann et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)

These laws have two limits for this decision. First, low validation loss is an imperfect proxy for social and longitudinal behavior. Second, the original fit assumes a data-rich regime; a small fixed corpus, repeated epochs, synthetic data, and different objectives are outside that simple regime. A 2026 preprint proposes correcting this with terms for unique-data scarcity and overfitting, but it is a new model fit to published grids, not yet a settled replacement law. Treat it as a reminder to measure the actual data/epoch curve, not as a planning oracle. [Bryant & Liu, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09189)

### 2.2 Small-data pretraining and synthetic/curriculum data

BabyLM makes low-data pretraining measurable. The 2024 challenge used 10M-word and 100M-word tracks and a multimodal track; submissions were compared on grammar, question answering, pragmatics, and grounding. Across 31 submissions, a hybrid causal/masked objective did best among the reported approaches; no multimodal submission beat the multimodal baselines. The authors found training FLOPs correlated strongly with average performance, and winning approaches changed data, objective, and architecture together—making attribution to any one ingredient difficult. [Hu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05149)

The new 2026 controlled BabyLM study is stronger evidence about objective/architecture choices because it holds corpus, tokenizer, model-size family, schedule, and evaluation wrapper fixed while varying token mixers and six generative objectives. Across 432 completed grid runs, autoregressive Transformers were strongest at every tested scale: peak fine-tuning macro scores 64.88 (10M-word track) and 66.94 (100M-word track), with peak BLiMP scores 64.70 and 75.13. Those are scores on the study's public tasks, not general intelligence or a human comparison. [“What Survives the BabyLM Budget?”, ACL Anthology, 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.7/)

Architecture can matter within the budget. In 2026, a parameter-matched study trained 21 hybrid Gated DeltaNet + grouped-query-attention models from scratch on BabyLM's 100M-word corpus. Weight-tied depth recursion improved held-out loss by 0.0195–0.0252 nats for two matched pairs, with three seeds per pair; the domain-balanced 17.4M-token dev set showed the same direction in all 36 model/domain/seed cells. The result is conditional on a **matched data-exposure** comparison: under matched compute, the non-recursive twins trained to 1B tokens used two-thirds the recursive models' FLOPs and beat them by 0.0088–0.0117 nats. This is a data-efficiency trade, not free efficiency. [Sasidhar, 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.10/)

Synthetic data can make a narrow target tractable. TinyStories introduced short, simple stories generated by GPT-3.5/GPT-4 and showed very small models can generate coherent stories under that restricted vocabulary and genre. This is a proof of *target restriction plus curated supervision*, not evidence of spontaneous adult-like social behavior. [Eldan & Li, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)

The phi-1 result is stronger but still narrow. Microsoft reported a 1.3B code model trained for four days on eight A100 GPUs using 6B “textbook quality” web tokens and 1B synthetically generated textbook/exercise tokens; it scored 50.6% pass@1 on HumanEval and 55.5% on MBPP. A 350M sibling scored 45% HumanEval under the same pipeline. This establishes that data quality and synthetic instruction-like examples can yield strong narrow code performance at smaller parameter counts. It was not trained on a child-scale corpus, did not learn from scratch on the stated hardware, and was not a general human-like agent. [Gunasekar et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11644)

The BabyLM pragmatic evaluation is a direct caution against treating low-data grammar results as human-like competence. Askari et al. (2025) tested BabyLMs trained on under 10M and under 100M tokens against five Gricean maxims, children, and a 3T-token LLM. The under-100M-token models generally outperformed under-10M models, yet remained below child-level and LLM performance. [Askari et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.4/)

### 2.3 What children receive is not a word-count contest

The oft-cited estimate that children hear 30 million fewer words by age four across socioeconomic groups came from a small observational sample and extrapolation. Sperry, Sperry & Miller's re-examination observed 42 children aged 18–48 months in five US communities, combining ethnography and longitudinal home observation. They did not support the original gap claim and found that excluding multiple caregivers and bystander speech systematically undercounts exposure for some children. Child input is variable and socially situated; “100M words” is a useful benchmark budget inspired by acquisition research, not a precise universal child's training corpus. [Sperry et al., 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cdev.13072)

Models also consume a different kind of evidence. A model may train on curated text, books, web pages, synthetic explanations, and repeated examples. A child gets speech paired with gaze, joint attention, objects, action consequences, turn-taking, correction, prosody, and a continuous stream of embodied situations. BabyLM's 100M-word multimodal challenge is only a limited approximation: in 2024 its multimodal entrants did not beat baseline models. The evidence does not show that simply copying a child's word count reproduces the child's learning conditions.

### 2.4 What “behaves like a human” must mean here

For a model trained from scratch, the claim must be made at the *system level* and decomposed. A practical evaluation would need to show: (1) persistent internal state across episodes; (2) state that is learned from interactions rather than scripted case-by-case; (3) controlled causal influence of that state on later choices and language; (4) social and pragmatic competence across unseen situations; (5) stable identity under noise, conflict, and time; and (6) useful calibration and disclosure. A language model plus external brain can meet some criteria even if its weights are frozen. A scratch-built model alone does not imply them.

Existing methods show partial pieces:

- **Recurrent/state-space language models.** Mamba reports its 3B model matching Transformer models twice its size on its language pretraining and downstream evaluations, with linear-time sequence processing; training still parallelizes over sequences, while inference maintains recurrent state. RecurrentGemma/Griffin offers 2B and 9B sizes, combining recurrence and local attention with fixed-size recurrent state and reported comparable performance to same-size Gemma baselines. These make persistent *sequence state* practical; neither gives a persistent autobiographical self across application sessions by itself. [Gu & Dao, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752); [Botev et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07839)
- **Memory built into language modeling.** Memorizing Transformers use a k-nearest-neighbor memory of recent key/value activations; performance improved with memory up to 262K tokens and helped on code/math tasks with test-time-defined functions or theorems. RETRO trained a 7.5B-scale generator with a 2T-token retrieval database and reported GPT-3/Jurassic-1-comparable results on The Pile with 25x fewer parameters. These are explicit retrieval mechanisms, but they rely on large external stores/retrievers and are not themselves a human autobiographical learning theory. [Wu et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08913); [Borgeaud et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)
- **Interactive memory and reflection.** Generative Agents used a general LLM plus experience logs, reflection, retrieval, and planning for 25 agents in a sandbox town. Ablations supported the contribution of observation, planning, and reflection to evaluator-rated believability. The model weights were not trained from scratch for the agent. CLIN used persistent textual causal memory with a frozen language model in ScienceWorld; authors report +23 absolute points over Reflexion on repeated task trials, +4 zero-shot transfer points to new environments (+13 for new tasks), then +17 (+7) from continued memory updates. These show architectural memory can improve task adaptation without weight updates; they do not establish human social behavior. [Park et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442); [Majumder et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10134)
- **Affect and drives.** Cognitive-architecture research has long implemented explicit drives, appraisal and emotion signals, typically through rules or utility/reward shaping. I found no primary evidence in this pass that a sub-1B general text model trained from scratch with affect signals in its objective thereby gains robust human-like affective behavior. This is an evidence gap, not evidence that the approach cannot work. AI_friend's own model-independent, interpretable affect state may be a better experimental starting point than entangling affect with language-model weights.
- **Continual learning.** Updating model weights online remains vulnerable to forgetting and instability. A 2026 ICML position paper argues for modular memory combining fast in-context accumulation with slower in-weight updates; it is a position/framework, not a validated universal solution. CLIN is concrete evidence for memory-based adaptation; stable online weight learning for an ongoing conversational identity remains an open engineering/science problem. [Dorovatas et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01761)

### 2.5 Evidence for and against small scratch models

**Strongest evidence in favor:** small models can beat or approach larger baselines when the task and data are tightly constrained. BabyLM has 2024 systems around 50–100M parameters that perform competitively on its restricted benchmark; the 2026 controlled study shows the Transformer objective/architecture remains a strong baseline, and 2026 recursion experiments show measurable data-efficiency gains. TinyStories shows coherent generation at tiny scales on synthetic stories. Phi-1 shows 1.3B can be strong on coding with a curated 7B-token mixture and 8 A100s. None directly compares a <1B scratch model with a prompted general model on longitudinal human-like behavior.

**Strongest evidence against:** the low-data models remain far from child-level pragmatic performance; small models need narrow distributions and careful data engineering to look competent; scale/data curves continue to predict lower language-model loss with more compute; and interactive-agent results with persistent memory typically use a capable pre-trained LLM rather than training the whole model from scratch. Thus a narrow scratch model may beat a general model on a defined role benchmark, but there is not yet strong evidence that it can match the general model's breadth, robustness, or conversational fluency. The direct comparison requested remains **unsettled** because a matched benchmark and head-to-head study do not appear in the opened sources.

## 3. State of the art

| Method/system | Reported scale and result | What it establishes | What it does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| BabyLM 2026 controlled grid | 10M/100M words; 432 completed runs; AR Transformer peak macro 64.88/66.94, BLiMP 64.70/75.13 | Controlled small-data objective/architecture comparisons | Human-level behavior or cross-domain generality |
| BabyLM 2026 weight-tied recursion | 21 GDN+GQA models; 3 seeds per pair; loss gain 0.0195–0.0252 nats at matched data exposure | Compute-for-depth can improve low-data next-token learning | Better behavior, lower total compute, or faster inference |
| TinyStories | Tiny models on synthetic, child-vocabulary stories | Data/task restriction can make small generation coherent | Conversation, adult knowledge, or social competence |
| phi-1 | 1.3B; 7B tokens; 8 A100s × 4 days; 50.6 HumanEval pass@1 | High-quality and synthetic code data helps narrow capability | Cheap training or a human-like mind |
| Mamba / RecurrentGemma | Mamba-3B; Griffin 2B/9B | Recurrent or hybrid sequence state is viable at language-model scale | Cross-session identity or autobiographical memory |
| Memorizing Transformer / RETRO | kNN memory to 262K tokens; RETRO with 2T-token database, 25x fewer parameters than GPT-3 | Explicit retrieval can buy language/modeling performance | Small standalone system or psychologically grounded memory |
| Generative Agents / CLIN | 25 sandbox agents; ScienceWorld benchmark for CLIN | Persistent external memory and reflection can shape later behavior | Scratch-trained language model; open-world human behavior |

The practical state of the art for this project is not a single “human-mind model.” It is the combination of a capable language model with external state, retrieval, and evaluation. Scratch training makes sense as a long-term architectural research program, while using test-vehicle models to establish the interfaces and measures first.

## 4. Where our design is weak or out of date

### Model assumptions in the current code

The current model boundary is partially abstracted, but the application still assumes prompt-oriented, text-generating LLM behavior:

- `backend/app/llm/__init__.py` defines `LLMClient.generate`, `generate_stream`, `describe_image`, and health/close. This is provider-neutral but not model-family-neutral: the interface is string prompt → string or token stream. A model with a typed state update, recurrent cache, action distribution, or multimodal interaction loop needs a richer capability contract and per-session state lifecycle.
- `backend/app/cognitive/decision.py` asks an LLM for intent, goal and inferred valence/arousal in a prompt; it requests `<thought>`/`<think>` tags, then extracts a JSON object from text and falls back to a default intent. The parsing path assumes generated prose can be made into a schema by extraction. It also takes inferred affect from the language model after the synchronous affect update in the current pipeline (see V3 architecture account), which misses the opportunity for a causal affect update on that turn. This connects to V3 `NF-12` (user words currently fail to move affect), `NF-19` (the named RPE is a turn-surprise heuristic), and the affect rows `NF-11`–`NF-21`.
- `backend/app/cognitive/action.py` assembles identity, retrieved memory, state, vision, and social-inference text into prompts and streams a reply. `_compute_endocrine_options` maps cortisol to sampling temperature, dopamine to `top_p`, and fatigue to output token count. That is a hand-designed linkage from named affect variables to sampler knobs; it does not show a learned causal relation between affect and behavior. Preserve it as a falsifiable baseline, not as established human biology.
- `backend/app/cognitive/identity.py` renders the persona as a text prompt. This fits a prompted foundation model, but a scratch model could instead consume identity as persistent, typed latent state, a learned conditioning vector, or an external policy state. Keep the authored core and slow drift outside the model so that changing model weights cannot silently rewrite identity.
- `backend/app/state/memory_store.py` hardcodes `nomic-embed-text` for Ollama embeddings and validates a 768-dimensional vector. The memory store therefore assumes an external embedding model and a fixed vector space. An in-model embedding head would require an adapter contract and either versioned vector spaces or re-embedding/migration. Keep semantic memory records independent of any particular vector representation.
- `backend/app/cognitive/core.py` builds proactive text prompts from the identity prompt, mood label, energy, memories and a subconscious thought. It assumes the language model is the realization engine for internally generated thought. A future model can plug in if the brain supplies a structured thought/action intent and consumes generated output through the same boundary.

The current code's strongest path toward model replacement is the typed Python/Rust contract layer and a provider client protocol. Its weak point is that **capability and state contracts stop at text**: parsing, generation, sampler options, image description, and embeddings are still model/provider-specific. The V3 fidelity ledger's named problems also warn against calling mechanisms human-like by naming: `NF-1/NF-2` for ACT-R trace/retrieval assumptions, `NF-6` for unwired temporal memory, `NF-12` for affect not driven by the user's words, `NF-19` for heuristic RPE, `NF-23`–`NF-25` for trust/relationship measures, and `NF-39` for novelty from a short lexical window. These are system-state/modeling problems; they will not be solved just by replacing the LLM.

### The “much easier” claim

**Verdict: contradicted for end-to-end development, partly plausible for objective/control fit.** Scratch training gives control over tokenizer, recurrence, state-conditioned objectives and data curriculum. That can avoid working around a model trained for broad web prediction. But the evidence does not show that a small bespoke model can recover broad language and pragmatic competence cheaply. The small-data results are benchmark- and distribution-specific; BabyLM's pragmatic result is below child-level, and phi-1's favorable narrow-task result used seven billion tokens and eight A100s. A better testable claim is: “A small scratch model may make the state transition mechanism more inspectable and improve one pre-registered role metric under a fixed budget.”

## 5. Limits

Every limit is classified using the required labels.

| Limit | Class | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No scientific consensus on what internal organization is sufficient for human-like minded behavior | `SCIENCE` | There is no agreed mechanism or benchmark that reduces this system-level target to one model architecture. |
| Whether any artificial system has subjective experience | `UNKNOWABLE` | The report's target excludes phenomenal experience; no available behavioral test settles whether a model feels. |
| Single GPU with 8 GB VRAM and an M5 with 16 GB unified memory cannot train frontier-scale models or reproduce 7B–trillion-token pretraining | `RESOURCE` | Those runs require much more memory, compute, and training data. |
| Estimate of sustained throughput and wall-clock on the owner's specific M5/GPU | `ENGINEERING` | Not benchmarked here; a short fixed-shape throughput probe can settle it. |
| Data licensing, deduplication, tokenizer quality, and corpus provenance for a child-like interaction corpus | `ENGINEERING` | Known data-engineering work; must be completed before pretraining. |
| Broad child input volume and composition | `SCIENCE` | Estimates vary by population, definition of child-directed speech, observation method, and inclusion of bystanders/multiple caregivers. |
| Human-like conversational competence from small data alone | `SCIENCE` | Controlled small-data studies show selected capabilities but not full transfer to natural social interaction. |
| Persistent online weight updates without forgetting and identity drift | `SCIENCE` | Continual learning remains unresolved; a safe split between memory writes and slow in-weight consolidation is still an empirical choice. |

| Comparing scratch model to prompted general model fairly on “human-like role” | `ENGINEERING` | Needs frozen tasks, prompts, budgets, blinding, and longitudinal measures; the evidence base does not supply this comparison. |

### Smallest realistic scratch project at stated scale

**Scope:** a language-modeling and interaction-loop pilot, not a humanoid mind.

- **Data collection:** start with a permitted, source-attributed corpus inspired by child-directed language plus simple dialogue/narrative; deduplicate and split by source and episode. Do not scrape private conversations. A 10M-word BabyLM-style corpus is an available research pattern; word-to-token conversion depends on corpus/language/tokenizer. A practical target is **10–30M unique tokens**, with a second fixed-budget synthetic set only as a separately labeled arm.
- **Tokenizer:** train a 4K–8K unigram or BPE vocabulary on training data only; report unknown/fragmentation statistics on held-out child-directed and adult dialogue. Compare byte/character fallback only if multilingual coverage is in scope.
- **Model:** a 10–30M parameter decoder-only Transformer baseline, plus at most one recurrent/weight-tied variant at matched parameters. At small scale, prioritize a reliable end-to-end run and comparison over architecture breadth.
- **Training:** autoregressive next-token loss, 1/2/5/10 epochs over 10–30M unique tokens. This gives 10M–300M token exposures; the chosen point must be reported, not called “child equivalent.” Add synthetic dialogue or next-state/appraisal prediction only as a separately ablated objective.
- **Compute:** approximate training FLOPs as `6 × parameters × training tokens`. Examples: 10M × 10M tokens ≈ 6×10^14 FLOPs; 30M × 30M ≈ 5.4×10^15 FLOPs; 100M × 100M ≈ 6×10^16 FLOPs. These exclude tokenizer/data processing, validation, checkpointing, and architecture-dependent overhead.
- **Wall-clock:** until measured, use an explicit planning range. At assumed sustained 0.1–1 TFLOP/s effective training throughput, 30M parameters × 30M tokens (5.4×10^15 FLOPs) is roughly **1.5–15 hours ideal arithmetic**, or around **3–60 hours** at 25–50% effective utilization. This is a planning calculation, not an M5/8GB benchmark; actual throughput may be lower due to small batches, memory traffic, kernels, or CPU/GPU transfer. Start with a fixed 100-step throughput probe and extrapolate only after confirming stable loss and memory use.
- **Memory:** 30M FP32 parameters with gradients and Adam first/second moments is roughly 0.5 GB for those arrays before activations, buffers, and allocator overhead; mixed precision changes the accounting. Sequence length, batch size and implementation may dominate. The 8 GB card should be enough for a modest model if activations and batch sizes are controlled. A 100M model is possible experimentally but is a less useful first run under this corpus budget and hardware.
- **Evaluation:** token loss by source/domain; BLiMP-style syntax; controlled pragmatic probes; long-history retrieval/interference; paired longitudinal conversations; identity consistency and counterfactual state ablations; report confidence intervals across at least three seeds for architecture claims. Avoid claiming human parity from a single benchmark.

**What the pilot could show:** that this team can train, checkpoint, serve and evaluate a small model; whether the selected corpus supports learnable language patterns; whether a structured/recurrent state path changes a narrow behavioral metric; and actual throughput on these devices.

**What it cannot show:** that a few million tokens approximate a child's total experience; human-level pragmatic or social competence; consciousness; generalization to real life; or that scaling this small model will converge to frontier breadth. A pilot can disconfirm an overly ambitious recipe, not validate the end state.

### Cheaper alternatives and what each gives up

| Alternative | What it buys | What it gives up |
|---|---|---|
| Continued pretraining of a small open model | Retains broad language priors; tests custom corpus/objective at lower compute | Not a genuinely from-scratch model; inherited tokenizer, objective, and priors confound claims |
| Distillation from a general teacher | More useful narrow behavior per student parameter; BabyLM 2024 HGRN2 distillation improved its benchmark macro score | Knowledge/behavior is teacher-shaped; synthetic teacher errors and licensing; does not test human sample efficiency |
| LoRA/adapters | Cheap role adaptation and reversible experiments | Core representations remain pretrained; not a new model architecture or independent identity |
| Recurrent/SSM or weight-tied design | Smaller recurrent inference state, long-sequence efficiency, or extra effective depth per parameter | Does not remove data/compute needs; training may be slower; results depend on task and hardware kernels |
| External modular memory with frozen model | Fast persistent experience and inspectable state; CLIN/Gene­ra­tive Agents show useful patterns | Model does not absorb memories into its weights; quality depends on retrieval, memory governance, and language-model capability |
| Mixture of specialists or modular components | Allocate capacity to narrow subskills and update modules independently | Routing, evaluation and integration complexity; total expert capacity can exceed VRAM; not necessarily lower training compute |

## 7. Open questions for the owner

1. What narrow behavior should be the first scratch-model win: conversational pragmatics, stable identity expression, autobiographical continuity, or another outcome? Evidence cannot rank these by product value.
2. Is “trained from scratch” specifically about all language weights, or may the target use an externally pretrained embedding/tokenizer/teacher during data creation? That choice changes what the experiment can claim.
3. Which data sources and language(s) are acceptable for a child-like corpus, given licensing, privacy, and the difference between spoken interaction and curated text?
4. Should the long-term model learn continuously in its weights, or should experience remain in external memory with rare consolidation? Current evidence cannot settle the stability/plasticity tradeoff for this product.
5. What minimum human comparison would justify saying the system “behaves like a human” in a bounded domain while retaining AI disclosure? No existing benchmark decides that product threshold.
