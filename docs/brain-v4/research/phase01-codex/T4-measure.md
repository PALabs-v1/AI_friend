# T4 — Measuring “how close”

**Evidence cutoff: 28 October 2026.** This report treats “closer” as better measured functional organization: named internal variables persist across time, update from relevant experience, and predictably change later behavior. It does not operationalize consciousness or claim that social ratings measure it.

## 1. Bottom line

There is no defensible single “mind-likeness” number. A composite would hide the difference between accurate memory, affect that changes action, self-initiated behavior, and observers attributing a mind. Report a **profile** with independent automatic and human-rated endpoints, and a separate causal test for each mechanism the architecture claims to add. A state variable counts as functionally integrated only when its value survives the relevant interval, changes a later behavior in the preregistered direction under controlled intervention, and has a measurable consequence beyond the wording of the prompt.

Existing work gives useful pieces, not a validated whole-brain scale. LongMemEval and LoCoMo probe factual and temporal memory; EmoBench probes emotion understanding/application; persona and Turing studies show that prompts alone can produce convincing traits or human-like judgments; observer questionnaires measure perceptions, not architecture. Newer 2026 work moves toward long-horizon event probes and stateful social simulations, but its benchmarks remain simulation-bound and do not establish that any particular internal state caused the behavior.

For AI_friend, the strongest near-term test is a preregistered within-scenario comparison of intact, reset, sham-edited, and counterfactual-swapped internal state, with fixed model, prompts, and task inputs. Log state transitions and later behavior. Include a prompt-only state rendering control. Keep the model fixed within each experiment; deployment model comparisons are a separate factor (V4 D3). Human judgments are a second, blinded outcome, never the sole proof.

The current architecture already exposes potential measurement hooks—state snapshots, affect/trust updates, proactive eligibility and outcome traces—but several named mechanisms are known to be defective or unwired. In the V3 grounding audit, NF-12 says user-event appraisal reads the agent’s own mood, NF-23 says trust updates are driven by the agent’s mood, NF-16 says affect decay uses nominal rather than elapsed time, NF-6 says temporal contradiction handling is unwired, and NF-39 identifies a novelty/habituation defect. These are excellent measurement targets: a “mind-like” label cannot substitute for passing the input-to-state-to-behavior trace test.

## 2. Evidence

### A measurement profile

Use automated probes on every run and human ratings on a sampled, blinded subset. Automated results answer what the system did and whether records support it; humans answer whether the behavior reads as coherent, appropriate, and mind-like. Keep both columns visible. Human ratings are not a ground-truth substitute for state or causality.

| Dimension | Automatic measure | Human judgment | What counts as evidence, and key confound |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Memory truth and temporal updating** | Fact-level precision/recall; temporal ordering and supersession accuracy; source/provenance hit rate; abstention when unsupported; contradiction resolution after a controlled update | Blinded rating of whether recollection is accurate, appropriately qualified, and used at the right time | Score against held-out interaction histories with answer provenance. Separate recall from reasonable inference. LongMemEval and LoCoMo supply useful task types, not evidence of autobiographical continuity. |
| **Affect dynamics** | State-response sign and magnitude against the preregistered event; lag; recovery/decay curve; repeated-event habituation; stability under equal elapsed time; state-to-action effect size | Whether the response and later behavior are emotionally coherent for this relationship and event, without relying on emotion words alone | Score sequences, not isolated emotion labels. Distinguish the agent’s state from its estimate of the user’s state. Emotion QA benchmarks do not validate persistent affect. |
| **Drives and goal persistence** | Goal recall, priority ordering, persistence across interruption/restart, achieved/abandoned-goal accuracy, conflict resolution, and calibrated confidence | Whether priorities appear coherent over time and whether the system recognizes when a goal should yield | Keep goal success separate from stylistic persuasiveness; predefine when a goal is relevant. |
| **Initiative and timing** | Eligible-opportunity precision/recall; useful-contact rate; inappropriate/duplicate contact rate; time-to-initiation; cooldown and quiet-period violations; proportion of initiated actions with a traceable state/goal antecedent | Whether an initiation feels timely, relevant, and self-initiated rather than scripted or intrusive | Count opportunities, not just sent messages. Include negative-control situations where silence is correct. Product turn-taking rules remain hard constraints. |
| **Identity stability and slow drift** | Invariance of choices across paraphrased prompts and sessions; immutable-core violation count; adaptive-trait drift per episode; cross-session trait prediction; recovery after conflicting persona cues | Blinded consistency ratings across separated episodes, plus judgments of whether change feels gradual and evidence-based | Test fixed-core and adaptive traits separately. Persona prompts can manufacture consistency; compare prompt-only with persistent-state conditions. |
| **State-to-words/action coupling** | Paired intervention effect on preregistered choice, content, or timing; mediation trace from event → named state → downstream action; prediction lift from state over prompt/history baseline | Blinded forced-choice judgment of which response better reflects the stipulated prior state and context | Avoid scoring only explicit self-reports (“I feel…”). State ablation and plausible state swaps test causal dependency. Prompt-carried-state controls test whether the effect is just text. |
| **Self-monitoring and uncertainty** | Calibration (Brier score/reliability bins) for memory confidence, uncertainty-aware abstention, error detection and repair after feedback | Whether uncertainty is communicated proportionately and whether a correction is accepted without defensiveness | Treat verbal confidence as behavior to calibrate, not an introspective window. A calibration routine that is never invoked should score zero use. |
| **Persistence and time sensitivity** | State survival across restart; update latency; elapsed-time decay; equal-gap invariance under delayed ticks; causal influence at 1, 10, and 100+ turns / days | Whether continuity across long gaps feels stable without falsely implying a human past | Run simulated-time and wall-time conditions separately. Report persistence duration and gaps. “Long horizon” needs explicit turns, elapsed time, and event density. |
| **Perceived agency, experience, and social presence** | No automatic measure can establish subjective experience. Report only behaviorally grounded indicators (unprompted initiation, flexible planning, global availability of selected state) as separate counts | Mind-perception agency and experience items, social-presence / co-presence scales, and open-ended attribution | These scores reflect observer judgments and framing. Always disclose AI status; never optimize deception or infer feeling from a high rating. |

For every row, publish the task distribution, base model/checkpoint, prompt version, state variables exposed, duration, seed, denominators, confidence interval, and failure examples. A profile should show baseline and intervention results side by side, not average them into “closeness.”

### Evaluation families and validity

**Turing-style tests and believability.** A preregistered two-party test had participants converse for five minutes and classify the interlocutor: GPT-4 was judged human in 54% of trials, actual humans in 67%, and ELIZA in 22%. This measures short-interaction human-likeness under that protocol, not state persistence or causal organization. In a 2026 preregistered three-party study, GPT-4.5 with a persona prompt was judged human 73% overall across UCSD undergraduates and Prolific participants, versus 36% for GPT-4.5 without persona; in the Prolific sample, corresponding rates were 75.5% (111/147) and 42.1% (32/76). A separate 15-minute replication’s persona-prompted GPT-5 was judged human 59.3% (89/150). It costs a human panel and controlled conversation protocol. The prompt effect is a direct warning: passing the test can be an effect of the model’s role prompt, with no evidence of a persistent identity mechanism. Because AI_friend must disclose it is AI, “mistaken for human” is also the wrong product target. [Jones & Bergen, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08007); [Jones & Bergen, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2524472123).

**Mind perception and social presence.** Gray, Gray, and Wegner used ratings of 13 targets and 18 mental capacities across surveys from 2,040 unique respondents (including a sociable robot); factor analysis distinguished Experience (capacity to feel) and Agency (capacity to act/plan). These factors correlated with liking, saving, and wanting to make the target happy (r=.38–.72), so they are meaningful observer-attribution measures; they do not validate the target’s internal properties. Social-presence self-report scales ask about perceived co-presence, responsiveness, and mutual understanding. Scale results must be interpreted in their interaction medium and framing: a 2025 meta-analysis of 53 online higher-education studies found pooled effects varied significantly by delivery mode and instrument. This is adjacent validity evidence rather than a validation study of AI mind-likeness. A short questionnaire is low cost; recruitment and repeated longitudinal administration raise cost. These scores belong in the human-judgment column, with condition and interface reported, not in the causal evidence column. [Gray et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1134475); [Mykota, 2025](https://doi.org/10.55667/10.55667/ijede.2025.v40.i1.1351).

**Long-horizon memory.** LongMemEval has 500 human-curated questions covering extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, and abstention. The paper reports about a 30% accuracy drop for evaluated commercial assistants and long-context LLMs when information must be remembered across sustained interactions; this is benchmark-specific, not a universal memory rate. LoCoMo conversations average 588 turns, 27.2 sessions, and 16,618 tokens, over a few simulated months; tasks cover QA, event summarization, and multimodal dialogue generation. It uses machine-generated conversations edited by humans, which makes it scalable but less ecologically representative than real relationships. Local runs avoid per-call hosted-inference cost, but may take appreciable local inference time; answer grading, prompt construction, and memory-access conditions affect comparability. Scores show recall and reasoning performance, not that the system has a continuous self. [Wu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813); [Maharana et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.747/).

**Persona consistency.** PersonaLLM assigned Big Five profiles to GPT-3.5/GPT-4, measured responses to the 44-item Big Five Inventory, and rated story outputs automatically and by humans. Humans identified some traits with up to 80% accuracy; that accuracy fell significantly when evaluators were told the author was AI. This exposes both a prompt effect and an evaluator-framing effect. Newer AgentPersonaBench (Oct 2026 preprint) tests unannounced traits through observable behavior across survey, chat, web, and app tasks: 2,460 tasks over 867 traits, with up to 84.7% full-pass adherence on leading models under its conditions. It is a promising behavioral test, but evaluates persona-driven user simulation and not persistent identity arising from an internal state store. Benchmark inference cost ranges from low for survey-like offline probes to moderate/high for interactive web/app setups and human ratings. Keep prompt-only/persona-conditioned baselines in every V4 identity experiment. [Jiang et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.229/); [Huang et al., 2026, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04379).

**Emotion and affect.** EmoBench is 400 hand-crafted English/Chinese questions about emotion understanding and application, deliberately requiring inference about causes and social dilemmas. Its human comparison involved 48 recruited adults (24 English-task and 24 Chinese-task participants; task allocation n=12 per language-task pair), aged 21 or older and screened on a quiz. GPT-4 scored below the average human on the reported benchmark. This is evidence about scenario-based emotional reasoning, not a persistent affect mechanism. A 2026 benchmark, Sentipolis, instead tests stateful PAD agents, dual-speed dynamics, emotion-memory coupling, and network-level social outcomes across thousands of simulated interactions; its abstract reports model-dependent believability gains and some norm-adherence costs. That is closer to the V4 question, but simulation outcomes and evaluator choices limit external validity. [Sabour et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.326/); [Fu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.368/).

**Integrated long-horizon probes.** REMP (October 2026) creates 80 dialogue trajectories, each averaging about 60 sessions and 800 turns, for 160 characters; its anchor-based probes test relationship, emotion, memory, and persona on 11 LLMs. It offers an efficient template for event-triggered V4 probes, but remains role-play simulation. DynToM (ACL 2025) includes 1,100 contexts, 5,500 scenarios, and 78,100 questions about temporal evolution of beliefs, emotions, intentions, and actions. Ten LLMs underperformed humans by 44.7% on the paper’s aggregate; this does not establish the same gap for AI_friend or real-world interactions. [Yu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.602/); [Xiao et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1171/).

**Consciousness-indicator checklists.** Butlin et al. derive computational indicators from recurrent processing, global workspace, higher-order, predictive-processing, and attention-schema theories. This is a theory-informed structural audit, not a validated consciousness test. Its conclusions are provisional because theories compete and indicators can be behaviorally imitated. Report which structural indicators are present, absent, or not assessed, and state that this does not settle subjective experience. [Butlin et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708).

## 3. State of the art

Best available for this track is **compositional evaluation**, not a single benchmark:

1. **REMP** is the closest new benchmark shape to V4’s event-loop question: long trajectories with targeted probes for memory, emotion, relationship, and persona. Its 80 trajectories average 60 sessions/800 turns; 11 LLMs were evaluated. It is a test harness for observable continuity, not state causality.
2. **LongMemEval + LoCoMo** are useful established memory controls. Their 500 questions and 27-session / 588-turn conversations, respectively, cover factual, temporal, update, abstention, and retrieval dimensions. Run a local snapshot with fixed model and memory budget; do not claim scores are directly comparable across different model/judge setups.
3. **Sentipolis** is the strongest newly published 2026 example of an explicit persistent-affect and emotion-memory architecture evaluated across thousands of social-simulation interactions. The reported effect varies by model capacity and includes a social-norm tradeoff. It argues for reporting capability and side effects separately; it does not prove subjective affect or human external validity.
4. **AgentPersonaBench** improves on trait self-report by hiding the target trait and scoring actions across four interaction surfaces. Its 2,460 tasks / 867 traits and up to 84.7% full-pass result make it a useful design reference for covert behavioral probes. It remains a preprint and studies persona conditioning, not autobiographical identity.
5. **Theory-derived consciousness indicators** are appropriate only as a separately labeled structural inventory. They are not a ranking score for V4 and cannot support a claim of felt experience.

For human ratings, use blinded pairwise comparisons with response order randomized and reversed across raters. A 2025 systematic evaluation of 15 LLM judges, 22 tasks, roughly 40 answer-generating models, and more than 150,000 evaluation instances found position bias that varied with judge, task, and quality gap. Therefore, an LLM judge is a screening tool, not the sole primary endpoint; report human agreement, order-flip rate, ties, and judge version. [Shi et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18/).

## 4. Where our design is weak or out of date

- **The V3 architecture description already places an asynchronous System-2 appraisal after the synchronous affect update.** `docs/brain-research-v3/00-current-architecture.md` says stage 5 updates affect before stage 6 intent classification and the background appraisal arrives too late to inform that update. This makes latency, event-to-state ordering, and delayed-cause tests essential; a module diagram alone overstates the loop.
- **Affect currently risks measuring itself rather than the event.** V3 finding NF-12 identifies self-mood as the appraisal input, which predicts self-reinforcing or absent responses instead of event-grounded affect. A benchmark that only asks “what emotion is this sentence?” misses this; test sign, timing, recovery, and later choice after controlled event input.
- **Trust and temporal memory require counterfactual and update probes.** NF-23 identifies trust updates driven by the system’s mood; NF-6 says the contradiction/reconsolidation mechanism is unwired. A static retrieval score misses whether evidence revises state and later action. Include adversarially paired evidence, superseded facts, provenance, and a counterfactual trust swap.
- **Personality consistency is especially vulnerable to persona-prompt inflation.** V4 decision D5 specifies an authored immutable core with slow drift, while D3 makes the model a per-deployment choice. Report prompt-only and model-only controls; evaluate stable choices after the persona prompt is withheld, across sessions and model tiers. A higher perceived trait score under a stronger prompt does not show identity state.
- **The V4 product rules make human-impersonation outcomes invalid.** D4 requires AI disclosure; the owner’s working definition excludes claims of human memories/body. D1’s preference for higher Experience attribution (in `docs/brain-v4/06-decisions.md`) should be measured as observer perception separately. A high Experience score is not evidence that experience exists and should not be produced by hiding disclosure.
- **A single believability score would duplicate the known observer cue problem.** The Turing literature shows persona prompting and interface framing can move judgment substantially. Do not use a Turing pass or social-presence score as the acceptance criterion for “closer”; use it as one human-facing profile item and disclose the exact framing.
- **The V3 fidelity workflow is a good precedent but should be extended from mechanism fidelity to causal fidelity.** `docs/brain-research-v3/12-neuroscience-grounding.md` says neuroscience proposes and behavioral benchmarks decide, and ties NF rows to code, prediction, and probe. V4 should add a named state→behavior intervention result to each new mechanism’s traceability record.

## 5. Limits

- **UNKNOWABLE — subjective experience.** There is no agreed observable test that can settle whether a system feels. The report can assess structural hypotheses and observer attribution, not phenomenal experience. Say “not assessed / not decidable by these methods,” not “the system feels” or “the system cannot feel.”
- **SCIENCE — what functional organization is sufficient for a mind.** There is no accepted complete theory that maps architecture or behavior to mindhood, and consciousness theories disagree about indicators. Improvements in a profile support only the named capacities.
- **SCIENCE — external validity of simulated social life.** Current benchmarks use scripted or generated interactions. Their event coverage and annotation can be strong while leaving real-world relationship dynamics, culture, and months-long use unresolved.
- **ENGINEERING — attribution of behavior to persisted state.** This is testable and feasible here with state snapshots, matched interventions, prompt controls, and trace logging; it is not yet the default evidence standard.
- **ENGINEERING — reliable affect, memory and initiative measurement.** Current V3 fidelity findings show concrete measurement targets and likely failure modes. They need well-designed local probes and immutable test histories; “brain-inspired” names are not validation.
- **RESOURCE — large external longitudinal cohort.** Internal raters and local simulation can establish initial engineering evidence, but cannot stand in for a diverse, long-duration user study. V4 D2 defers that study until internal evidence warrants it. Model tiers should remain fixed per experiment and reported, per D3.
- **ENGINEERING — judge and prompt artifacts.** Order, verbosity, disclosure, trait priming, and evaluator expectations can be randomized or controlled. A result that disappears when these are controlled is not evidence for state architecture.

## 6. What this means for V4

Ranked candidate moves:

1. **Make the state-to-behavior trace auditable.** Record event ID, pre/post named state, update reason/provenance, subsequent goal/decision/action IDs, timestamp, model/checkpoint, and prompt hash. **Outcome:** every claimed state influence in a held-out probe has a complete trace; unexplained/missing-edge rate and state serialization loss are measured. **Depends on:** stable state schema and trace retention.
2. **Use a causal intervention matrix for each new state mechanism.** Run four paired conditions on identical inputs: (A) intact state; (B) target state reset/removed; (C) sham edit matched for format/size but preserving target value; (D) plausible counterfactual swap of one named value. Add prompt-only state text (backend state cleared) and state-only-without-persona-text controls. Randomize run order, isolate instances, blind raters, and include negative-control scenarios where the state should make no difference. **Outcome:** necessity (A>B), specificity (A>C), predicted directional sufficiency (A vs D), and no false positive on negative controls. **Depends on:** deterministic replayable histories and plausible, in-distribution state values.
3. **Build a compact longitudinal probe suite.** Adapt task shapes from LongMemEval/LoCoMo/REMP: stale-fact update, elapsed-time gap, interrupted goal, ignored initiative, corroborated slow persona drift, neutral/positive/negative affect events, and restart/recovery. **Outcome:** fact provenance and supersession, correct behavior after long gaps, appropriate initiative, stable-core/slow-drift separation, and calibrated abstention by dimension. **Depends on:** fixed scenarios and objective rubrics that do not require an LLM judge.
4. **Add a preregistered human-rating panel.** Recruit roughly 30–40 raters for a pilot, each rating 12–20 randomized matched pairs, with balanced conditions and AI disclosure. Rate coherence, appropriateness, perceived agency, perceived experience, and social presence as separate constructs; include an open-ended reason. Use at least two independent raters per item and report ICC/agreement before interpreting mean scores. **Outcome:** human-rated differences align directionally with the measured behavioral profile, with uncertainty and agreement reported. **Depends on:** ethics/consent, fixed presentation, an adequate number of items, and budget. The pilot estimates variance/ICC; it does not justify a definitive population claim.
5. **Freeze a model-tier comparison protocol.** Run the same frozen evaluation under one small local, one larger local, and the chosen local Claude Code model where feasible; never alter the model inside a causal comparison. **Outcome:** architecture effect is reported within model and interaction is reported, not averaged away. **Depends on:** accessible model versions, hardware/runtime budget, and recorded decoding configuration.
6. **Keep observer perception bounded by honesty.** Ask about agency/experience only after plain AI disclosure and report those ratings as observer beliefs. **Outcome:** the architecture can improve causal/behavioral profile without increasing mistaken-human attribution or claims of human history. **Depends on:** wording that separates perceived capacities from claims of subjective feeling.

### Preregistered acceptance rule for “closer”

Do not let a broad profile become an untestable bundle. For each confirmatory intervention, preregister **one primary behavioral endpoint**, one named state variable, one predicted direction, one smallest effect of practical interest (SESOI), exclusions, stopping rule, model/checkpoint, prompt, seed protocol, and analysis. Treat scenario/history—not turn, token, or repeated rating—as the independent sampling unit. Estimate uncertainty with scenario-cluster bootstrap or a hierarchical model.

**Small research-budget proposal:** start with 30–40 matched scenario clusters for a feasibility pilot and human-rating reliability/variance estimates. For a confirmatory paired comparison, target about **66–70 independent scenario pairs** for roughly 80% power at two-sided α=.05 to detect a paired standardized effect around *d*<sub>z</sub>=0.35, before clustering or multiplicity. This is a planning approximation, not a literature-derived universal cutoff; calculate exact power using the actual outcome distribution and scenario clustering before preregistering. If compute or time permits fewer, publish the achieved sensitivity / minimum detectable effect and confidence interval instead of calling a null “no benefit.” Lakens recommends justifying sample size from the inferential goal and SESOI, rather than choosing a conventional round N.

**Provisional gate:** for one primary causal endpoint, require (i) intact-vs-reset improvement in the predicted direction with the 95% CI excluding zero; (ii) a counterfactual state intervention produces the preregistered direction; (iii) sham and negative controls stay within the preregistered equivalence margin; and (iv) the effect persists without explicit persona/state text in the prompt. Set SESOI before data; choose it from a concrete behavior (e.g., improved supported-fact retention, correct goal priority, or fewer inappropriate initiations) and baseline variance, not after seeing results. Adjust alpha or designate a single primary test when testing multiple state mechanisms. A non-significant superiority test alone is **inconclusive**. Call “architecture did not produce a practically meaningful effect in this test” only if the CI lies fully within a preregistered equivalence interval ±SESOI, or the estimated effect is reliably harmful. If the model improves only under persona priming, an LLM judge, or explicit state narration—and the effect vanishes under controls—that is evidence of prompt/judge artifact, not of an internally state-driven loop. [Lakens, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267).

## 7. Open questions for the owner

1. Should perceived **Experience** remain a goal (V4 D1) once it is explicitly separated from behavioral function and consciousness claims? This is a product/ethics decision, not one the measurement literature can settle.
2. What user-visible behavior defines the SESOI for V4: better memory, coherent initiative, affect-guided choices, stable identity, or a specified combination? Evidence can estimate effects but cannot choose their value weights.
3. What longitudinal duration is enough for an internal acceptance claim before the external 120-person study planned by D2: simulated months, 12-week internal use, or both?
