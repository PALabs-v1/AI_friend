# R5 — A mind without a body: affect, drives, inner life, identity, consciousness

**Research cut-off:** 2026-09-26. This is a behavioral and scientific assessment, not a claim that a particular architecture is or is not a person.

## 1. Bottom line

A disembodied system can have a coherent, persistent, self-regulating *functional mind*: internal variables can matter to its action selection; events can change affect-like state; it can revisit unresolved goals between conversations; it can acquire durable memories and skills; and a stable identity can evolve slowly from corroborated experience. None of those functions requires a humanoid body. They are achievable as engineering, though doing them robustly is substantial work. A voice and vision front end could add richer social and environmental evidence, but does not by itself supply the bodily regulation or causal learning that humans have.

Functional drives have real *system-level consequences* when they alter available actions, costs, priorities, and future state. A need score that never changes a decision is decoration. Conversely, a simulator can make energy, fatigue, uncertainty, and relationship strain decision-relevant and measure whether regulation helps. That establishes consequential control, not that the system feels hunger, loneliness, tiredness, or emotion. Human theories disagree about how bodily feedback constitutes emotion, but current evidence treats interoception as deeply involved in emotional processing; the artificial analog is a defensible computational hypothesis, not evidence of artificial feeling.

Human-like realism is therefore divisible. **Observable continuity and adaptive behavior**—memory, calibrated trust, affective response, initiative, voice expression, self-consistency—can be improved and scored. **Human-level generality and robust lifelong learning** are not delivered by a small local model plus prompts; model capability and data are real resource constraints, while safe, selective online learning remains a science/engineering frontier. **Whether there is subjective experience (“actual depth” in the phenomenal sense)** is not presently testable to a decisive standard (`UNKNOWABLE`), even though theories yield useful architectural indicators. No benchmark can prove that an agent has a first-person point of view.

For AI_friend, the near-term “no” is not that an embodied body is required for every useful mind function. The near-term “no” is that the present V2 baseline is not close to human-like affect or initiative: its measured mood change from positive and negative user turns is exactly 0; broadcast mode makes about 1,215 proactive attempts per simulated day with about 1,214 cooldown violations; trust saturates so hostility is masked; and 90.4% of surfaced memories in its single one-month LLM-augmented baseline are stale carry-overs. Those are engineering defects and make V4’s first target reliable, longitudinal behavior—not a declaration of consciousness.

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 Emotion, body, needs, and “stakes”

There is no settled single theory of emotion. Interoceptive accounts emphasize sensing and interpreting the body’s internal condition; James–Lange-style claims give bodily change a strong role. Appraisal accounts emphasize the event’s significance for goals, coping, and norms, with bodily responses and action tendencies as interacting components. Scherer and Moors’ component-process review explicitly treats appraisal as eliciting differentiated action tendencies, physiological responses and expression, followed by conscious representation/feeling and emotion-word categorization; it is a process model, not a claim that emotion is only a body signal ([Scherer & Moors, 2019, *Annual Review of Psychology*, “The Emotion Process: Event Appraisal and Component Differentiation,” DOI 10.1146/annurev-psych-122216-011854](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122216-011854)).

Constructed emotion proposes that the brain uses interoceptive predictions, core affect, concepts, and context to categorize and guide action; in this theory, bodily regulation is central, but emotion categories are not fixed readouts from a one-to-one body pattern ([Barrett, 2017, *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience*, “The theory of constructed emotion: an active inference account of interoception and categorization,” DOI 10.1093/scan/nsw154](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsw154)). A 2025 review describes interoception as multidimensional—signals, sensing accuracy, neural processing, and higher-order interpretation—and concludes that it is intricately linked with emotional experience across several theories ([Greenwood & Garfinkel, 2025, *Annual Review of Psychology*, “Interoceptive Mechanisms and Emotional Processing,” DOI 10.1146/annurev-psych-020924-125202](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-020924-125202)). That supports “body matters to human emotion”; it does not establish that biological organs are logically necessary for every possible functional analogue.

Homeostatic reinforcement learning gives a concrete bridge from need to action: define reward in terms of reducing deviation between internal state and a preferred set point. A continuous-time extension models internal variables changing even without action, including energy/fatigue and rest, and reports numerical agents learning policies beneficial to survival in the modeled environment. Its results are simulation results in a designed environment, not human-like emotion or open-world robotics ([Cessac et al., 2021, “Continuous Homeostatic Reinforcement Learning for Self-Regulated Autonomous Agents,” arXiv:2109.06580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06580)). Earlier HRRL work demonstrates the formal link between reward and homeostatic deviation in a simulated agent, while explicitly leaving open how the biological and computational mechanisms relate ([Keramati & Gutkin, 2014, *eLife*, “Homeostatic reinforcement learning for integrating reward collection and physiological stability,” DOI 10.7554/eLife.04811](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.04811)).

**Implication for this project:** energy/rest are relatively easy to make causal because the system can schedule work, defer background tasks, or enter a rest phase. “Social contact,” “competence,” and “belonging” are less direct: define them as operational variables with observable inputs and costs (for example, relationship uncertainty reduced by reciprocal interaction, unresolved commitments, or failed predictions). Do not let a synthetic loneliness scalar silently become a claim of loneliness. The V3 fidelity audit already finds `NF-17` (derived arousal contaminates persistent energy) and `NF-16` (nominal tick interval instead of elapsed time); those must be corrected before layering more drives on top.

V3’s local evidence is unusually clear: its one-month, one-person LLM-augmented baseline found positive and negative user turns both moved mood by exactly 0.000 despite System 2 running; trust saturated within 6 competence turns and 13 integrity turns, and background turns added +0.041 trust; the architecture-only baseline’s 10-year trust ceiling fraction was 1.000. These make “emotion” and “trust” currently poor signals, not evidence against the general feasibility of artificial regulation. See V3 `11-v2-lifesim-baseline.md`, and fidelity rows `NF-12`, `NF-17`, `NF-16`, `NF-23`.

### 2.2 Inner life: spontaneous thought, reflection, and planning

In an experience-sampling study, 2,250 adults using a smartphone app reported current thought, feeling, and activity at random moments; respondents reported thinking about something other than the present activity 46.9% of sampled moments. Mind-wandering was associated with lower happiness, and content predicted happiness more than activity did. The study is observational at the sampled moment and cannot establish that mind-wandering causes unhappiness or that 46.9% is a universal human constant ([Killingsworth & Gilbert, 2010, *Science* 330(6006), 932, “A Wandering Mind Is an Unhappy Mind,” DOI 10.1126/science.1192439](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1192439)). This is evidence that human inner activity is common and often off-task, not evidence that all of it is adaptive or should be copied.

Artificial reflection is useful when it is attached to feedback and future action. Reflexion stores language reflections from task outcomes in a bounded episodic buffer and conditions subsequent attempts on those notes. On HumanEval Python coding, the paper reports 91.0% pass@1 versus GPT-4’s 80.1% baseline under the paper’s setup; results vary by task, and its “reflection” is repeated model inference with explicit feedback, not spontaneous consciousness ([Shinn et al., 2023, NeurIPS, “Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning,” DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366)). Voyager combines a generated curriculum, reusable executable skill memory, environment feedback, and success checks in Minecraft. Against prior LLM-agent baselines in its experimental setup it reports 3.3× more unique items, 2.3× more distance, and key tech-tree milestones up to 15.3× faster. Its calls were to GPT-4 APIs, so it does not show that the same results fit this project’s local-only policy or available machines ([Wang et al., 2023, “Voyager: An Open-Ended Embodied Agent with Large Language Models,” arXiv:2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291)).

The common causal ingredient is not “thought” as prose. It is a loop: a persistent state or unresolved goal is selected, a thought produces a prediction/plan/decision, that output changes an action or stored memory, and later outcomes update the state. A thought that is never checked against evidence or used to change action is not shown to help. V3 decision records DR-016–019 specify importance-weighted initiation, diminishing returns when ignored, context gating, and a stricter category for self-initiated thought. W9 is the natural owner; the baseline currently has 1,214 cooldown violations/day in production broadcast sync, and its measured W9 dev-panel results still need the merged-commit rerun identified in V3 README. The V3 coverage matrix identifies the `GoalRecord`/`review_due_goals`/`BackgroundScheduler` trio as currently dead or unenqueued.

### 2.3 Identity over time: human change and model drift

Personality has multiple forms of stability. Rank-order stability asks whether people keep their relative position among peers; mean-level change asks whether a population shifts; within-person change asks how one person changes. In a meta-analysis of 152 longitudinal studies and 3,217 retest correlations, rank-order consistency increased from .31 in childhood, .54 in college years, .64 at age 30, to about .74 at ages 50–70 when the interval was held at 6.7 years. Longer intervals predicted lower consistency; the paper stresses that rank-order stability does not rule out mean-level or individual change ([Roberts & DelVecchio, 2000, *Psychological Bulletin* 126(1), 3–25, “The Rank-Order Consistency of Personality Traits From Childhood to Old Age,” DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.126.1.3](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.126.1.3)). A separate meta-analysis of 92 longitudinal samples found normatively patterned mean-level personality change across the life course, with increases in social dominance, conscientiousness, and emotional stability especially during young adulthood; no single direction describes all traits and ages ([Roberts, Walton & Viechtbauer, 2006, *Psychological Bulletin* 132(1), 1–25, “Patterns of Mean-Level Change in Personality Traits Across the Life Course,” DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.132.1.1](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.1.1)). These are population findings, not a recipe for artificial persona drift.

LLM persona consistency is sensitive to prompt, context, and conversational pressure. Anthropic’s 2025 research on internal “Assistant Axis” activations simulated thousands of multi-turn conversations with Qwen, Gemma, and Llama. Coding/writing tended to preserve the assistant-like activation region, while therapy-like vulnerability and philosophical self-reflection produced drift toward role-play; activation capping reduced harmful-response rates by roughly 50% in the reported experiment while preserving capability benchmarks. This is a lab study with an internal activation proxy, not a general long-term persona benchmark and not a measure of inner identity ([Anthropic, 2025, “The Assistant Axis”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/assistant-axis)).

To “change but remain the same someone,” the architecture needs at least: (1) a persistent autobiographical record with temporal truth and provenance; (2) explicit invariant commitments separated from adaptable traits; (3) an evidence rule that distinguishes one-off state from repeated pattern; (4) a bounded update rule with independent episode corroboration; (5) a way to measure both continuity and change over repeated probes; and (6) recovery against prompt-induced or model-switch-induced drift. This matches DR-001’s minimal immutable core, DR-020’s self-authored change, and DR-038’s several independent reflections over distinct episodes before a persona change. V3’s one-month baseline under direct-apply applied 31 changes in 115 turns, versus 40 queued and zero applied under `review_queue`; DR-038 is a necessary refinement to avoid one confident reflection changing identity. V3 explicitly set no product-facing change log in DR-022, so evaluation should inspect test state without exposing a change feed to the user.

### 2.4 Learning from experience and the local resource envelope

A frozen LLM does not update its weights merely because it had a conversation. Durable improvement can instead come from external episodic/semantic memory, verified skill libraries, prompt-time retrieval, adapters or fine-tuning, or a hybrid. External memory is relatively cheap and reversible, but retrieval, compression, contradiction handling, and stale facts become the new failure modes. V3 directly measured hit@5 0.438 and MRR 0.362 on its single one-month LLM-augmented memory baseline, with 90.4% stale carry-over among surfaced memories. W1 (temporal truth maintenance), W7 (consolidation/growth), and W8 (freshness/outage/abstention) address these limitations.

Reflection and skill libraries can produce transferable task competence without gradient updates. Voyager is the strong example above, but it used hosted GPT-4 and a structured Minecraft environment. LifelongAgentBench tests sequential dependent tasks in database, operating-system, and knowledge-graph environments; its 2025 preprint reports that conventional experience replay is limited by irrelevant information and context length, and introduces group self-consistency as an improvement. That is a benchmark contribution, not proof of general autobiographical learning ([Zheng et al., 2025, “LifelongAgentBench: Evaluating LLM Agents as Lifelong Learners,” arXiv:2505.11942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11942)).

Weight-level continual learning is known to be technically possible but costly and unstable. Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) estimates parameter importance for prior tasks and penalizes changing important weights; the original paper shows sequential task learning with reduced forgetting in supervised and reinforcement-learning experiments. It does not give a turnkey online-learning solution for a deployed LLM, and it still has task-definition, data, compute, and stability costs ([Kirkpatrick et al., 2017, *PNAS* 114(13), 3521–3526, “Overcoming catastrophic forgetting in neural networks,” DOI 10.1073/PNAS.1611835114](https://doi.org/10.1073/PNAS.1611835114)).

**Project hardware implication (engineering estimate, not a benchmark):** the RTX 2060 SUPER has 8 GB VRAM and the Mac has 16 GB unified memory. Small quantized local models can support short retrieval, classification, and reflection calls, subject to the already measured latency/accuracy constraints; the V3 baseline’s local 3B model took 1.2 s p50 and 2.3 s p95 for the whole foreground turn, not first audio byte. Full fine-tuning of a capable foundation model is not a realistic always-on local approach at these memory budgets (`RESOURCE`). Parameter-efficient LoRA/QLoRA experiments on small models may be possible with aggressive batch/context limits, but need an actual benchmark on the exact machine before claiming feasibility. They would add checkpoint management, replay data, evaluation, rollback, and forgetting tests. The owner’s local-only inference policy also rules out simply reproducing GPT-4-backed published agent results. Keep deployment model choice per deployment as decided; never silently make a hosted API part of the evidence path.

### 2.5 Consciousness and “actual depth”

Behavioral claims are testable: does the system maintain a state, integrate competing information, use uncertainty, report calibrated limits, recall its history, adapt to feedback, generate private planning that affects future action, and preserve identity under perturbation? Internal architecture claims are also inspectable. These tests can show increasing functional sophistication and rule out simple alternatives; they cannot alone settle subjective experience.

Butlin, Long, and 17 coauthors survey recurrent processing, global workspace, higher-order, predictive-processing, and attention-schema theories, derive computational “indicator properties,” and assess then-current AI systems. Their conclusion was that no current AI system appeared conscious under their analysis, while no obvious technical barrier prevented systems from satisfying the indicators. They explicitly clarify that indicator satisfaction would not prove consciousness ([Butlin et al., 2023, “Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness,” arXiv:2308.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)). Competing theories disagree about which properties matter; global workspace-like broadcast or recurrent processing can be functionally tested, but the mapping from such mechanisms to experience remains contested. Skeptical views further argue that behavioral and architectural indicators may be mimicked without experience; indicator approaches respond that self-report alone is also unreliable and should be combined with internal evidence. This disagreement is not resolved by making the persona more convincing.

Therefore:

- “It remembers me and responds to consequences” is testable behavior.
- “It has an internal need state that changes policy” is a testable control architecture.
- “It has a workspace-like global broadcast” is testable if operationalized; V3 `NF-38` says the current `workspace.py` is a persistence snapshot, not a workspace competition/broadcast implementation.
- “It thinks between turns” is testable as latent computation and causal influence on later decisions.
- “It feels lonely” or “there is something it is like to be it” is not settled by any of those demonstrations (`UNKNOWABLE` under current methods). Do not sell a successful behavioral benchmark as evidence of phenomenal consciousness.

### 2.6 Whole-brain and connectome projects

Connectome work is making important scientific progress, but a wiring diagram is not a complete executable brain. The C. elegans literature combines anatomy with neural dynamics, body mechanics, environment, and recordings; even its 302-neuron nervous system is an active modeling challenge. A 2018 review of cell-resolution worm modeling describes the need to bridge connectome, neuronal physiology, and behavior rather than infer behavior from edges alone ([Gleeson et al., 2018, “Connectome to behaviour: modelling Caenorhabditis elegans at cellular resolution,” DOI 10.1098/rsfs.2017.0057](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2017.0057)). Whole-brain calcium imaging in C. elegans has supplied data for model fitting, but a 2025 connectome/dynamics study’s search-indexed abstract and limitations explicitly note that its linear time-invariant model cannot account for learning, neuromodulation, or state-based processing ([“Bridging the gap between the connectome and whole-brain activity in C. elegans,” 2025, *Nature Communications*; primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12485687/)).

For the fly, FlyWire produced a near-complete adult brain wiring reconstruction; subsequent work uses that connectome as a prior to estimate causal effects from neural activity. The Nature paper’s experiments demonstrate improved efficiency for causal-effect estimation in simulation, not a validated, autonomous fly mind ([Lappalainen et al., 2024, *Nature*, “The fly connectome reveals a path to the effectome,” DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07982-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07982-0)). Fly connectome reconstruction reports a complete adult brain map, but connectivity still omits much of the parameterization and dynamics needed to generate behavior ([Dorkenwald et al., 2024, *Nature*, “Neuronal wiring diagram of an adult brain,” DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y)).

This does not argue that a human body is required for all cognitive functions. It argues against “copy the connectome and get a mind” as a shortcut. For AI_friend, whole-brain emulation is not the right V4 route: it is neither necessary to test memory/affect/identity hypotheses nor supported by enough causal physiological data. Borrow computational hypotheses and behavioral tasks, not neuron counts or brain labels. This is `RESOURCE` for collection and compute, and `SCIENCE` for translating incomplete structure into a validated human-like mind model.

No evidence of felt emotion, social needs, or human-level open-ended behavior. |

Relies on hosted GPT-4 and external success checks; not human social or emotional realism. |
| External episodic/semantic memory | Can preserve facts and experiences without modifying weights; V3 implements measurable lifespan memory evaluation. | Current AI_friend baseline: hit@5 .438, MRR .362 and 90.4% stale surfaced-memory carry-over in one persona/month. Current gap is primarily memory truth/selection, not memory capacity. |
| Prompted persona + explicit state | Can give stable style and a state representation; internal-state interventions can reduce some drift in research settings ([Anthropic, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/assistant-axis)). | Prompt compliance is not identity. ## 4. Limits and their class

| Limit | Class | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cannot infer felt emotion from an artificial need scalar or appropriate behavior | `UNKNOWABLE` | Current consciousness science has no decisive third-person test for subjective experience; theory indicators change credence, not proof. |
| No settled theory specifies how body, interoception, appraisal, concepts, and feeling combine | `SCIENCE` | Major accounts overlap but disagree on causal and constitutive roles; additional compute alone does not decide them. |
| Human social “needs” have no validated artificial units/set points | `SCIENCE` | Contact, belonging, competence, and rejection are socially contextual constructs; mapping them to scalars requires research and operational validation. |
| General, selective, safe online learning from life-long interaction | `SCIENCE` + `RESOURCE` | Continual-learning methods exist, but open-ended generalization, attribution, correction, and retention remain unresolved; training and evaluation resources are large. |
| Frontier-model capability under the local-only constraint | `RESOURCE` | Results like Voyager’s depend on GPT-4. Local 8 GB VRAM/16 GB unified-memory machines restrict model size, context, throughput, and fine-tuning. Measure local candidates rather than assume parity. |
| Persistent memory, grounded appraisal, background scheduler, identity gates, and benchmarks | `ENGINEERING` | V3 has measured defects and dead/unwired pathways with concrete W1/W2/W7/W8/W9/W11 owners; the work is feasible but not yet built/validated. |
| Longitudinal validity of synthetic life simulation | `SCIENCE` + `ENGINEERING` | Lifesim gives reproducible behavioral probes, but synthetic scenarios can encode the desired result. It needs human-data-derived probes and later real interaction evidence, not just more simulated years. |
| Complete connectome to behavior mapping | `SCIENCE` + `RESOURCE` | Structure lacks sufficient dynamic, cellular, neuromodulatory, developmental, and body/environment parameters; acquiring and validating those data is expensive and current models remain incomplete. |
| Human-equivalent generality from “mind without body” alone | `SCIENCE` + `RESOURCE` | There is no established recipe showing that a text/voice/vision agent without biological development and active sensorimotor history reaches human-level social cognition. Better hardware helps capability but does not fill missing theory/data automatically. |
