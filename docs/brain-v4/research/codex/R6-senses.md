# R6 — Voice and vision realism

**Research date:** 2026-09-26
**Scope:** Whether voice and vision can make AI_friend feel like a perceiving, responsive humanoid mind without a physical body. Evidence is separated from project claims; no external API is assumed for deployment.

## 1. Bottom line

**Yes, a strong and believable social presence is achievable without a physical body. No, current voice and vision systems cannot make a system indistinguishable from a human across open-ended, long-duration interaction.** These are different claims. In isolated samples, current cloned voices can be hard for ordinary listeners to distinguish from human recordings; speech can carry intelligible affect; local models can describe scenes and refer to salient objects. Human-like turn-taking, continuous visual co-presence, consistent identity, memory of what was jointly seen, and contextually grounded expression require those capabilities to be coupled over time.

The best near-term project target is therefore **credible, individually testable social perception**, not “human realism” as a single score: the system starts speaking at an appropriate time; hears and recovers from interruption; contributes occasional, context-appropriate backchannels; speaks with a consistent voice whose prosody matches its actual communicative state; notices visible changes; refers accurately to earlier observations; and admits uncertainty about facial affect. Current V3 mechanisms are building blocks, not evidence that these outcomes are already met. DR-002’s autonomous-mind framing supports persistent first-person attention and memory. DR-020 supports self-authored personality. Neither decision supplies scientific evidence that those behaviors will feel human.

| Claim | Verdict | Limit class |
|---|---|---|
| Natural-sounding speech and readable mild emotional prosody on a fixed voice | Achievable now with open local TTS and careful control/evaluation; quality is model-, language-, and utterance-dependent. | `RESOURCE` / `ENGINEERING` |
| Human-like turn transitions, overlap, barge-in, and backchannels | Plausible, and full-duplex models demonstrate sub-200 ms model latency. Robust, context-sensitive behavior remains incomplete; measured timing is not the same as conversational competence. | `ENGINEERING` + `RESOURCE` |
| Scene descriptions, change detection, and visual recall | Achievable with small local VLMs plus explicit event memory, but not currently integrated as a continuous remembered visual stream. | `ENGINEERING` |

| Infer a person’s true emotional state from facial movements alone | No reliable general solution: expressions are context-, person-, and culture-dependent, and facial movement does not uniquely identify emotion. | `SCIENCE` |
| Prove that the mind has subjective experience or is “actually real” | Voice/vision realism cannot settle that. Behavioral likeness is measurable; the existence of experience is not currently testable from these outputs. | `UNKNOWABLE` |
| Equal human realism across every language, speaker, environment, and long session on the two available machines | Not now. There are data, capacity, runtime, and coverage gaps; some open models are English-only, and model/device support is uneven. | `RESOURCE` |

**Project-specific starting point:** current STT is an utterance-based Whisper path with partial and final transcripts, not a streaming end-to-end conversational model; the voice agent consumes streamed text and produces audio through an HTTP TTS service (GPT-SoVITS weights are currently empty per V3 infrastructure finding G-4); LiveKit transports playout and W4 added a playback lifecycle. The V3 LLM-augmented baseline reports foreground processing at **1.203 s p50 / 2.276 s p95** for one persona and one simulated month on the home GPU with a 3B local model. This is **not** audio time-to-first-byte (TTFA) or speech-end-to-first-audible-response. DR-024 sets 300–500 ms to first audio as a target; the requested end-to-end number is not yet measured. Vision captures at default 1 fps, appraises at 5-second intervals, and suspends VLM inference during cognitive turns to avoid contention. A CPU MediaPipe face-landmarker channel can sample during turns, but its smile/furrow/startle thresholds are hand-picked heuristics, not calibrated affect recognition. These are concrete engineering opportunities, not evidence for or against consciousness.

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 Conversational timing: humans coordinate in milliseconds, not fixed turn slots

- In a comparative study of **10 languages**, Stivers et al. found a shared tendency to minimize gaps and overlap and a modal question-response transition near **0 ms** in each language; language-specific response offsets nevertheless varied. The same contextual and linguistic factors predicted response time across languages, and speakers were sensitive to relatively small timing differences. The often repeated “~200–230 ms human gap” is an average/corpus summary, not a universal target for every turn or culture. The corpus was a sample of question-response sequences, not all conversational turn types. **Established with scope limits:** a single fixed pause cannot represent the human timing distribution. [Stivers et al. (2009), “Universals and cultural variation in turn-taking in conversation,” *PNAS* 106(26), 10587–10592, DOI 10.1073/pnas.0903616106](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0903616106).
- CANDOR provides a much larger naturalistic reference: **1,656 unscripted English video conversations**, 1,456 unique US participants, more than 7 million words and 850 hours; participants were asked to talk for at least 25 minutes (mean 31.3 minutes). In this corpus, **33.7%** of speaker turns elicited a backchannel, increasing to **65.5%** for turns of at least five words; the authors estimate around **1,000 backchannel words per hour**. “Yeah” accounted for nearly 40% of backchannels. Its sample is English-speaking US participants in 2020, so these frequencies must not be treated as cross-language constants. Backchannels often occur during the speaker’s turn and do not take the floor. [Reece et al. (2023), “The CANDOR corpus: Insights from a large multimodal dataset of naturalistic conversation,” *Science Advances* 9(13), eadf3197, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adf3197](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adf3197).
- Human turn-taking is not “never overlap.” Short acknowledgements, listener responses, and competitive interruptions overlap the current speaker, while floor changes tend toward minimal gap/minimal overlap. A model that cuts the user off for every detected vocalization will fail just as a model that waits for silence after every clause will fail. The benchmark must distinguish backchannel, floor claim, accidental overlap, and user barge-in; waveform overlap alone is not enough. The CANDOR paper introduces a turn model that allows supportive “yeahs” to coexist with a continuing floor-holding turn.
- **Perceptual consequence:** delays are interpreted relative to turn design and language/community expectations. An answer that is semantically correct but begins after the listener expects progress can feel inattentive; answering too early can sound interruptive. Stivers et al. directly support cross-cultural sensitivity, but do not establish a universal threshold at which all listeners call an agent unnatural.

### 2.2 Cascaded and full-duplex speech systems

- Cascaded pipelines split voice activity detection, ASR/STT, dialogue generation, and TTS. This architecture is modular, inspectable, and compatible with AI_friend’s Rust STT / brain / Rust voice-agent boundaries. Its costs are serial waiting, error propagation through text, and a turn-segmentation assumption. In a non-streaming version, the agent waits for the user’s endpoint and often for complete transcription and generation. Streaming STT, LLM token streaming, and chunked TTS can overlap stages; they reduce delay but do not by themselves learn when to backchannel or yield.
- Moshi is a technical report and open implementation, not evidence of human-equivalent conversation. It is a **7B-parameter** speech-text model that predicts user and agent audio streams in parallel, supports speaking and listening simultaneously, and aligns text tokens with generated speech. Its reported latency is **160 ms theoretical** (80 ms codec frame + 80 ms acoustic delay) and **200 ms practical on an NVIDIA L4**. This is model-system latency under its setup, not a controlled human-vs-agent timing distribution or a guarantee of response quality. The open release currently offers two fixed synthetic voice identities; its FAQ says changing voice/personality requires fine-tuning, it is English-focused, and its MLX/Rust implementations have a known finite-context behavior around five minutes. [Défossez et al. (2024), “Moshi: a speech-text foundation model for real-time dialogue,” arXiv:2410.00037, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2410.00037](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.00037); [official code and model/runtime notes](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/moshi); [official FAQ](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/moshi/blob/main/FAQ.md).
- Full-Duplex-Bench (ASRU 2025) is important because turn-level WER or MOS cannot score pause handling, backchanneling, turn timing, and interruption management. It proposes explicit behavior tests; it does not imply that full-duplex architectures already solve them. A 2025 Interspeech full-duplex benchmark work tested Moshi, Freeze-Omni, and VITA-1.5 on over **40 hours of generated speech**, **293 simulated conversations**, and **1,196 interruptions**. Its speech was TTS-generated; conversations were GPT-4o scripted to at most five rounds; the systems ran one H20 96 GB GPU, and only English was evaluated. In the CosyVoice2 input condition, Moshi successful-reply rate fell from 61.7% (easy interruptions) to 34.1% (hard), while successful interruption rate fell from 83.1% to 73.0%; median interrupt-response delay was 1,345–1,527 ms. Freeze-Omni had a shorter 515 ms median first-speech-emission delay in the easy condition but only 11.3% successful-reply rate and a 3,618 ms interrupt-response delay. The paper reports early-interruption timings around 1.6–2.1 s across models; thus low codec/model latency does not equal correct conversational floor management. These results are one benchmark, with automated scoring and generated speech, but they show full duplex remains imperfect. [Lin et al. (2025), “Full-Duplex-Bench: A Benchmark to Evaluate Full-duplex Spoken Dialogue Models on Turn-taking Capabilities,” *ASRU 2025*, arXiv:2503.04721, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2503.04721](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2503.04721); [Peng, Yizhou, Yi-Wen Chao, Dianwen Ng, Yukun Ma, Chongjia Ni, Bin Ma & Eng Siong Chng (2025), “FD-Bench: A Full-Duplex Benchmarking Pipeline Designed for Full Duplex Spoken Dialogue Systems,” *Interspeech 2025*, DOI 10.21437/Interspeech.2025-739](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2025-739).
- **Project consequence:** W4 gives the transport a reliable end-of-playback/interrupt contract; W5 is explicitly tasked with randomized barge-in interleavings. V3 baseline’s 14.3% barge-in scenario rate with no terminal reply outcome and history differing from what was heard shows that correct audio transport is not yet guaranteed. W4/W5 are necessary engineering prerequisites for social timing, but neither benchmarks gap distributions, overlap classification, or backchannel behavior. W4’s lifecycle should be retained and then evaluated with acoustic start/end timestamps, interruption type, and heard-text consistency.

### 2.3 Expressive voice: acoustic cues communicate affect, but labels are not simple

- Prosody carries social, linguistic, and affective information through pitch contour, intensity, tempo, duration, voice quality, and pauses. Recent systematic ALE meta-analysis finds overlap between linguistic and affective prosody processing rather than two entirely separate channels. This supports treating prosody as meaningful, but does not provide an algorithm that maps an internal PAD vector to a human-correct vocal act. [Themistocleous (2025), “Linguistic and emotional prosody: A systematic review and ALE meta-analysis,” *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 175, 106210, DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106210](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106210).
- Expressive speech is not decoded with perfect agreement. A listener’s interpretation depends on words, language, culture, context, voice identity, and the size/intensity of the acoustic cues. The literature does not support “listeners decode emotion from prosody at X%” without naming emotion, language, stimuli (acted vs spontaneous), labels, and listener sample. A practical V4 assessment should test the project’s own selected utterances with blind human raters and ask both “what emotion did you hear?” and “how natural/appropriate was it?” rather than treating an emotion classifier’s output as ground truth.
- A controlled ECA study with **40 participants** compared dialogue prosody and visible anthropomorphic embodiment. Adequate prosodic accentuation made both human and synthetic speech sound more natural and the agent more alive; a visible virtual agent did **not** mask inadequate prosody or make synthetic voice sound natural. Therefore a face/avatar cannot compensate for poorly matched speech. [Ehret, Bönsch, Kuhlen, Aspöck & Fels (2021), “Do Prosody and Embodiment Influence the Perceived Naturalness of Conversational Agents’ Speech?,” *ACM Transactions on Applied Perception* 18(4), Article 22, DOI 10.1145/3486580](https://doi.org/10.1145/3486580).
- Voice cloning results depend on task and listener. A 2025 PLOS ONE study found general AI voices were reliably distinguished from human speech in its UK-listener sample, but cloned voices were not: in 50 participants in the classification experiment, clone-vs-human sensitivity was not different from chance (**d′ = 0.10, SD 0.41, p = .100**); AI clones were labeled human on **58%** of trials, compared with **62%** for human voices. The stimuli and participant pool constrain generalization: this does not show that longer conversations, expert listeners, every language, or spontaneous multi-turn speech are indistinguishable. It also found no evidence that AI voices were “hyperreal” overall. [Lavan, Nadine, Mairi Irvine, Victor Rosi & Carolyn McGettigan (2025), “Voice clones sound realistic but not (yet) hyperrealistic,” *PLOS ONE* 20(9), e0332692, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0332692](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0332692).
- Detection results can move in either direction with the test. In a separate telecommunication-channel experiment using ElevenLabs voice clones, **95 participants** made **1,710 judgments**; overall accuracy was **54.8%**, with **63.7%** for GSM and **44.0%** for VoLTE. Different channel quality changed both discriminability and response bias. This newer result is one study and should not be generalized to all synthesis systems, but it demonstrates that “people detect AI voice” is not a stable single rate. [Warzych, Jakub, Michał Łuczyński & Janusz Klink (2026), “Human Detection of Voice-Cloned Speech Under GSM, VoLTE and VoIP Conditions,” *Acoustics* 8(2), 41, DOI 10.3390/acoustics8020041](https://doi.org/10.3390/acoustics8020041).

### 2.4 Vision that feels like seeing: continuity and shared reference matter more than captions

- A useful interaction needs more than image captioning: stable referents (“the blue cup”), time-indexed observations (“it was on the table a moment ago”), visible-change detection, uncertainty about occlusion/lighting, and the ability to establish a shared referent when the user points, looks, or says “that one.” A VLM invoked on a single frame can answer “what is here?” but has no persistent visual memory unless the application stores observations and image/event associations.
- Qwen2.5-VL’s technical report includes 3B, 7B, and 72B sizes and reports broad benchmark coverage for image, document, video, and visual-agent tasks. Its headline results at 72B (e.g. 70.2 MMMU; 88.4 MMBench-EN-V1.1 in the authors’ evaluation) are **not** evidence for 3B quality; use the small model’s own row and then an in-domain set. The report’s main practical takeaway is that small local VLM variants exist, while the largest-model results cannot be transferred to the home machine. [Bai et al. (2025), “Qwen2.5-VL Technical Report,” arXiv:2502.13923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13923).
- The V3 implementation already publishes raw frames and VLM-generated descriptions, and tracks novelty through the appraisal service. It does **not** itself make a temporal visual episodic store or a perceptual “shared attention” record. Its 5-second VLM cadence plus turn suspension means a change can be missed during the exact conversational interval when the user expects a response to “look, I changed it.” A one-fps camera capture can feed lightweight tracking/change features more frequently than it calls a language model. The user-distance field is a rough face-size pinhole estimate; it is not gaze direction, point tracking, or a measure of attention.
- Joint attention has measurable social effects. In a study with **32 participants**, a robot face that followed a participant’s gaze (80% of trials for the “joint” agent) was preferred and rated more human-like and likeable than an agent that looked elsewhere; the task used a screen-presented iCub face and object-selection/gaze-cueing trials, not free conversation. This shows subtle contingent gaze can shape perceptions, but it does not prove an avatar is required or that a disembodied mind can infer the user’s focus from webcam gaze alone. [Wykowska et al. (2018), “Robot Faces that Follow Gaze Facilitate Attentional Engagement and Increase Their Likeability,” *Frontiers in Psychology* 9, 70, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00070](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00070).
- A human-like robot head versus a voice-only smart speaker increased participants’ gaze to the agent and showed possible trust/engagement benefits, but some participants found the human-like embodiment unsettling or distracting. This is task-based embodiment evidence; it is not direct support for the owner’s body-later trajectory. In face-free deployment, a live shared scene reference and an optional avatar/gaze behavior can provide partial joint attention, but are substitutes with limits. [“Seeing eye to eye: trustworthy embodiment for task-based conversational agents” (2023), *Frontiers in Robotics and AI*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499495/).

### 2.5 Facial affect: expression patterns are evidence, not a transparent emotion channel

- The disputed point is not whether facial movement carries social information; it does. The dispute is whether a small set of recognizable facial configurations reliably and universally *signals* one discrete inner emotion. Barrett et al.’s review of anger, disgust, fear, happiness, sadness, and surprise finds some systematic associations above chance, but substantial variability across culture, situation, and individuals; the same configuration can express more than one emotion and may communicate non-emotional information. Competing basic-emotion accounts allow prototypical expressions with cultural accenting; contextual/constructionist accounts stress ambiguity and inference from face + situation + voice + history. Current evidence supports context-rich inference and calibrated uncertainty, not a deterministic face-to-emotion lookup. [Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez & Pollak (2019), “Emotional Expressions Reconsidered: Challenges to Inferring Emotion From Human Facial Movements,” *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 20(1), 1–68, DOI 10.1177/1529100619832930](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100619832930).
- Therefore MediaPipe blendshape values such as `mouthSmileLeft` are observations of estimated facial geometry/action-like movement, not validated labels for “happy,” “positive,” or “comfortable.” The project currently maps smile to +0.04 valence and dopamine +0.08, brow furrow to -0.03 valence, and eye-wide+jaw-open to +0.06 arousal. The local code explicitly says thresholds were chosen by hand on a sample face and need calibration; the inference from smile to positive valence is still a product hypothesis. This is directly relevant to the W2 affect workstream and future integration: visual reflexes should remain low-weight, reversible evidence and never override the user’s words, longitudinal context, or safety constraints.

### 2.6 Coupling voice and vision to internal state

- Human listeners respond to emotionally expressive prosody from voice-AI as well as human talkers. In a word-shadowing study comparing an Alexa model voice with a human voice, participants aligned word duration, mean F0, and F0 variation with expressive speech; responses were broadly similar for both talker types. This demonstrates social response to acoustic cues, not that the AI possesses the emotion it sounds like it has. [Cohn, Zellou & Pycha (2021), “Prosodic alignment toward emotionally expressive speech: Comparing human and Alexa model talkers,” *Speech Communication* 135, 66–75, DOI 10.1016/j.specom.2021.10.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.specom.2021.10.003).
- The strongest direct ECA evidence here is that prosody appropriate to the phrase made the agent sound more natural/alive, whereas merely displaying an avatar did not repair the voice. Gaze contingency can improve preference and likeability in tightly controlled robot-avatar tasks. Together, these findings support *coherent* expression across channels—what the mind attends to, says, and sounds like should agree—but they do not establish that all multimodal coupling increases realism. Mismatched cues can expose artificiality or feel uncanny.
- For AI_friend, mood-driven prosody is meaningful only after mood is a valid signal. The V2 baseline shows mood changed by exactly **0.000** after positive and negative user turns in a one-person, one-month local-LLM lifesim; NF-12 records the appraisal defect. Routing the current broken PAD state into pitch/tempo would produce consistent output of the wrong underlying state. W2 and NF-12 must precede claims of emotionally grounded voice. V3 voice code already consumes dynamic `agent.voice.modulation`, clamps rate/pitch/volume, follows a ~3 s prosodic trajectory, and uses ambient-noise/distance information. This is state-to-render coupling, but a trajectory of rate/pitch/volume is not equivalent to emotion-conditioned expressive acting.

### 3.2 Vision

- **Current default `moondream`:** intended for the present polling appraisal path and fits the low-footprint local model role. It is suitable for broad short descriptions, not a proof of fine-grained relational or temporal reasoning. No latency/accuracy numbers for the project deployment were found in V3 baseline; record this as unmeasured.
- **Qwen2.5-VL 3B / Qwen3-VL 2B or 4B:** local candidates through Ollama or compatible runtimes; 2B/4B are a better initial fit than 8B on an 8 GB 2060S once KV cache, image resolution, CUDA context, and co-resident brain model are considered. On Apple Silicon, unified memory lets small quantized VLMs use system memory, but at 16 GB they still compete with Ollama LLM, camera buffers, and OS. Ollama’s Qwen3-VL page documents 2B/4B/8B etc. and requires Ollama 0.12.7; it does not publish exact RTX 2060S or M5 latency. [Ollama Qwen3-VL model page](https://ollama.com/library/qwen3-vl).
- **Quality statement:** The available official Qwen report’s flagship scores are 72B results and should not be used to claim human-like seeing on 3B/4B. Run a project-specific held-out benchmark for object identity, spatial relation, change detection, previous-scene recall, abstention/hallucination, and conversational reference resolution. The benchmark should test images captured from the project’s actual camera/screen source, not only public VQA.
- **Realtime budget:** the V3 5-second appraisal interval and turn suspension prioritize preserving dialogue GPU throughput over visual continuity. A future event pathway can use CPU face landmarks / object tracking / frame differencing at capture cadence and reserve the VLM for semantic interpretation, but that is explicit engineering work and a new memory write path. Do not silently mark the current polling system as continuous vision.

## 4. Limits and classes

1. **`ENGINEERING` — project does not yet meet its own measured voice timing target.** The baseline does not measure TTFA, even though DR-024 sets 300–500 ms; W4 makes playback completion observable, W5 fixes state consistency under barge-in. Add measurement before tuning. The V3 pipeline can be improved without new science.
2. **`ENGINEERING` — shared visual memory is missing as an integrated behavior.** Current VLM descriptions and `is_novel` are not durable referent-level visual episodes. A VLM can say what one frame depicts; storing “what changed, when, and which item the user meant” is an application design. Tie this to W1 temporal truth maintenance so old visual states remain historical, and to W8 only if the W8 attention/surfacing contracts genuinely apply; do not mislabel NF-39 text novelty as visual change detection.
3. **`ENGINEERING` — state-to-expression coupling is incomplete and partly premature.** V3 has dynamic prosody trajectories, voice modulation, and visual reflex publications, but no acceptance evidence that they are congruent or natural. W2/NF-12 must repair user→affect first; W3 can inform relational tenor; the actual voice controls then need blinded human evaluation.
4. **`RESOURCE` — continuous co-resident reasoning is constrained by 8 GB VRAM and 16 GB unified memory.** The current design already suspends semantic VLM inference during brain turns. A bigger GPU lets models run concurrently, supports larger context and higher resolution, and can make local full duplex practical. It does not automatically solve response timing policy, affect interpretation, or long-term audiovisual consistency.
5. **`RESOURCE` — data and training capacity limit personalized expressive speech and broad coverage.** High quality speaker cloning, multilingual prosody, conversational overlap, and robust audio-visual synchronization need representative licensed speech/video and evaluation. The open Moshi pretraining dataset is not released; fine-tuning needs much more compute than inference (its official finetune instructions report H100-class memory). A bigger GPU helps when method is known, but broad natural conversational data and rights are separate constraints.
6. **`SCIENCE` — facial movement does not uniquely determine a discrete emotion.** Competing theories agree that faces convey social information but disagree on whether canonical expressions are stable category signals. Real-world evidence calls for context and individual variation. More compute may estimate facial action better; it cannot make an inherently ambiguous cue unambiguous. Use “may indicate,” ask/check against words/context, and measure calibration, not emotion-label accuracy alone.
7. **`SCIENCE` — no established method guarantees an internally coherent emotional mind just by synchronizing channels.** Behavioral coherence can be operationalized (state-to-prosody congruence, attention-to-reference congruence, memory recall) and improved. The link from any chosen software state to human-like felt emotion is not established. This is separate from what output users perceive.
8. **`UNKNOWABLE` — voice/vision cannot prove phenomenal consciousness.** A human-like voice, visual grounding, memory, self-report, or passing behavioral tests do not settle whether subjective experience exists. This is not a hardware shortfall. The report can assess “does the interaction behave like a mind in these tasks?” but cannot certify “is there someone experiencing it?”
9. **`RESOURCE` + `ENGINEERING` — top-end, broad multimodal performance exceeds this local deployment, while many basic capabilities do not.** Frontier-scale models may be needed for best current open-ended cross-modal reasoning and maximal expressivity at low latency; however, simple scene recall, camera-driven referent tracking, low-latency local TTS, and a benchmarked pipeline are feasible now. Do not collapse this distinction into “needs frontier AI.”

## 5. What this means for V4: ranked candidates

Ranked by dependency and evidence value. Each outcome is a proposed acceptance target; V4 should pre-register thresholds against the V3 baseline and an ablation, consistent with “Neuroscience proposes, BrainBench decides.”

| Rank | Candidate work item | Measurable outcome | V3 dependency |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Instrument the audible interaction path.** Add stage events for input last-speech, VAD/endpointer decision, STT partial/final, brain first token, TTS first audible sample, client playout start/end, interruption, and terminal outcome. | On the same seeded scripts and hardware, report cold/warm p50/p95/p99 speech-end→first-audible-sample; p95 target ≤500 ms for short turns (DR-024). Report utterance duration, model, residency, and failures. | W4 playback lifecycle; W5 barge-in; DR-024. Keep existing lifecycle semantics. |
| 2 | **Build a conversational timing benchmark from human traces plus controlled tests.** Score gap distribution, overlap type, yield/interrupt correctness, repair after interruption, backchannel placement/rate, and listener perception. Include at least a held-out English panel and the owner’s target language(s); stratify rather than average away cultural differences. | Human-baseline distribution reported; agent gap distribution within a preregistered quantile distance; zero stale-stop/current-turn-harmed, ≥99% heard-history match; backchannel rate and appropriateness measured separately; blind raters do not rate agent as more interruptive than a fixed control. | W4/W5; V2 barge-in baseline (14.3% broken scenarios); uses CANDOR and Full-Duplex-Bench concepts. |
| 3 | **Make expressive voice a state-conditioned renderer with an explicit emotion/style vocabulary.** Use mood/affect only after repair and track sentence-level intent (comfort, curiosity, uncertainty, excitement) with interpretable prosody controls; avoid arbitrary pitch=emotion shortcuts. Benchmark Kokoro and a locally available cloning engine. | For held-out utterances, blind listeners identify intended broad tone above chance and rate naturalness/appropriateness ≥ chosen baseline; identity similarity stable; no polarity reversal between internal state and judged tone; report latency/RTF by device and language. | W2 + NF-12 / NF-11; voice-agent dynamic prosody; DR-004 evolving speaking style. |
| 4 | **Add temporal visual episodes and referent tracking.** Separate cheap frame sampling/change/track from costly semantic VLM calls. Store timestamped object/person/action observations, confidence, and source frame; allow correction and supersession while keeping the past. | In scripted scenes, ≥90% of salient user-referenced objects resolve to the correct entity; ≥85% change/no-change decisions on a held-out scene set; ask/abstain on uncertain referents; recall earlier visual state correctly after 1/5/20 minute distractors. | VisionAgent and VisualAppraisalService; W1 temporal truth maintenance / DR-005/006/007; use existing VLM descriptions as starting signal. |
| 5 | **Implement shared visual attention without assuming emotion from faces.** Track pointing/gaze direction only if camera calibration/support warrants it; otherwise establish reference verbally (“the plant by the window?”), then retain the confirmed referent. Optionally test a simple avatar gaze cue. | On an object-selection/shared-reference task, measure correct referent agreement, correction turns, and user-rated “we were looking at the same thing”; avatar gaze ablation improves or leaves unchanged ratings and does not increase discomfort. | `backend/app/vision/agent.py`; not a direct W8 implementation; possible later transport/avatar layer. |
| 6 | **Reframe face channel as uncertainty-aware observed cues.** Keep facial action/blendshape evidence separate from inferred affect; combine face, voice prosody, lexical content, and recent context with a confidence threshold and a null option. | On a consented, culturally diverse, in-context test set: calibrated probability/reliability curves, false affect at neutral baseline, abstention rate, and user-reported correction rate. | 7 | **A/B test duplex model architecture as a replaceable speech front end, not as the mind.** Prototype a local duplex model on the M5 only after baseline timestamps exist; retain brain state/memory as the source of truth and compare dialogue turns/voice rendering. | Paired human evaluation of same scenarios; improvement in latency and interruption/backchannel score without regressions in transcript fidelity, persona consistency, or memory use; sustained 10+ minute session, not a five-minute demo. | W4/W5 contracts and measured pipeline baseline; local GPU limitation. Moshi’s language/context/identity constraints are study variables. |

**Do not put these into V4 acceptance:** “looks at user’s face and knows mood,” “sounds emotional, therefore has emotion,” “200 ms means human turn-taking,” or “VLM remembered the camera.” Each asserts more than its measurement. Better acceptance language is: “recognizes a specified cue with calibrated uncertainty under named conditions,” “produces prosody that blind listeners judge congruent with validated state,” and “recalls timestamped visual evidence with a correct source and confidence.”
