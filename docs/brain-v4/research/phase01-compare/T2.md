Sizes: C 4.1k words, A 6.2k, L 7.5k. Headline: A answers a different question. Its evidence is almost all 2023 to 2025 and the brief asks what changed in 2026. C and L ground in 2026 sources I could open.

## 1. Agreements across all three

1. Phenomenal experience is untestable and not a target. C s5, A s5, L s5 (all UNKNOWABLE).
2. Verbal self-report is weak evidence. A: Butlin "cheap evidence". C: Koch on uncalibrated indicators. L: introspection "often an illusion", mimicry warning.
3. Sycophancy is a design hazard. C and L cite Cheng et al., Science 391 (49%, N=2405, matches the published version). A cites Sharma 2023.
4. D1 (pure goal, no dependence bound) needs instruments. Strength differs (see 2e).
5. D4 disclosure needs hardening. C: RealityTest. A: pre-generation filter. L: SB 243.
6. Persona drift is real; memory or a prompt alone does not fix it. C: ANCHOR 44.4%. L: Navarro. A: InCharacter, PersonaGym (weak, see 3).
7. Short-horizon or single-run scores mislead. C: Khanal 76.3 to 52.0 pass@1. A: tau-bench pass^k. L: Spencer horizon crossover.
8. The 16 GB Mac and 8 GB GPU cap model size. C: 30B does not fit. L: about 9B dense on the GPU box. A agrees in s5 but contradicts itself in 2b.
9. Two of three (C, A): stale facts and unwired `TemporalMemoryStore` (M-5, NF-6) are the main memory gap. L never lists NF-6.

## 2. Disagreements

a. Time frame. C and L cite mostly 2026 work. A's newest items are 2025 (Mem0, Seth) and its "2026 shifts" (R1-distill, Qwen2.5, Phi-4, Gemma 2, XGrammar, HippoRAG) predate the cycle. A does not answer question 1 of the brief. C and L are better.

b. Local models. A claims 14B at Q4 on the Mac at 22 to 26 tok/s and a 3B first token "under 85 ms on M5". No source; no cited paper could contain M5 numbers. C: verified Muse Glimmer 30B needs under 20 GB at 4-bit, so it exceeds both devices. L: Qwen3.5 and Gemma 4, with Artificial Analysis index and 80%+ closed-book hallucination for 4B and 9B. C and L better. L's point that prefill got cheaper but decode barely moved is the most useful design input (its source used a 24 GB machine, which L flags).

c. Memory direction. A: revive PPR graph activation, "dropping it was a step backward". C: add a long-context baseline, enforce memory status. L: rankings flip with horizon, write quality dominates (24% vs 2%). A rests on 2024 document multi-hop QA and ignores V3 ADR-001, where hybrid_rank lifted hit@3 from 0.02 to 0.69 (`docs/brain-research-v3/01-v2-delta.md:31`). A's "12 to 25 ms", "15 to 20%" and "obsolete_win >80% to <2%" are unsourced. C and L better.

d. Is JSON failure "obsolete"? A's rank 1 is grammar-constrained decoding, 95% to 0%. V3 `findings.md` F-005 calls the 95% "a model-compatibility finding, not a live production defect". A says decision.py uses bare regex; it calls `extract_first_json_value` and strips `</think>` (`decision.py:721-727`). Constrained decoding is still a cheap ENGINEERING option, but it is not 2026 news (XGrammar is Nov 2024) and A's framing is wrong. Neither C nor L covers it.

e. Strength on D1. A: "dangerous contradiction", uses Garcia v. Character Technologies as proof and says the court "ruled" strict liability in Oct 2024. Search shows a May 2025 motion-to-dismiss ruling letting claims proceed; allegations are not findings. L is best calibrated: randomized trials (N=981, 4 weeks; N=183, 21 days) show no average effect, harm is concentrated, no trial covered persistent state. C sits between.

f. Rank 1 move. C: enforceable memory status. L: state-intervention tests. A: constrained decoding. L's follows the owner's definition; C's is a recorded V3 gap and cheapest.
