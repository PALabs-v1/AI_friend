## 2. Disagreements

### "Much easier"
- Codex: contradicted overall, partly plausible for control fit. Offers a narrower claim: a scratch model may make state transitions more inspectable and win one pre-registered metric.
- AGY: "decisively contradicts", on three grounds: language tax, 2 bits per parameter, and an "optimization nightmare" for drives.
- Claude: splits general competence (contradicted) from the narrow persona case (silent), and cites the best pro-scratch evidence (Oh and Schuler).
- Best supported: Claude, then Codex. AGY overreaches.
  - Its "nightmare" paragraph rests on Keramati and Gutkin 2014 (rodent RL theory) and "CTCS-HRRL, 2024", which is in no citation list. Nothing shows labs omitted drives for stability.
  - Its F-005 evidence (95% parse failure) concerns prompted 3B models, not from-scratch ones. `docs/brain-research-v3/README.md:46` calls it "a model-compatibility finding, not a live production defect". AGY's "production models fail" framing conflicts with that.
  - None tests the other half of the owner's claim (no frontier model has what he proposes). Only Claude's size sweep (move 5) does, cheaply.

### Largest feasible model on 16 GB Mac plus 8 GB GPU
- Codex: pilot at 10M to 30M parameters on 10M to 30M tokens; 100M "possible experimentally". No ceiling stated. Ratio of about 1 token per parameter is far under Chinchilla's roughly 20.
- AGY: ceiling 100M to 250M (8-bit Adam plus checkpointing); project is 125M on 100M to 2B tokens.
- Claude: practical 124M to 200M on 2.5B to 10B tokens; 400M to 500M with 8-bit optimizers, flagged untested; 1B excluded.

Arithmetic (6 x N x D; 16 bytes per parameter for mixed AdamW):
- Codex FLOPs correct (6e14, 5.4e15, 6e16). Its 3 to 60 h for 30M x 30M is correct for its 0.1 to 1 TFLOP/s assumption, probably 10x too pessimistic for a discrete GPU. 0.48 GB state correct. Its text says "days, not hours" while its own numbers give hours.
- AGY correct: 2.3 h (125M x 100M), 9.6 d (x 10B), 625 d (135M x 600B), 16 GB for 1B. But 9 TFLOP/s is "30% MFU", i.e. an assumed 30 TFLOP/s peak, not measured. "Physical ceiling" is a heuristic: 8 GB / 6 bytes per parameter (bf16 weights and grads, 8-bit states) is about 1.3B before activations. Throughput, not memory, rules out 1B.
- Claude's seven-row table matches my script on every row (124M x 2.5B = 1.86e18; 7.2 / 3.6 / 2.2 / 1.4 days at 3 / 6 / 10 / 15 TFLOP/s; 1B x 20B = 463 to 93 days). 2 bits x 124M = 31 MB correct. Throughputs are labeled assumptions. Its llm.c anchor implies about 170 TFLOP/s per A100, plausible.
- Best supported: Claude. AGY's ceiling is defensible. Codex's pilot is cheap but too small to test conversation. Nobody measured either machine; only Claude notes Apple publishes no M5 training figure.

   - A: random weights, own tokenizer and data.
